If you've been off opioids

Your tolerance has dropped. The amount you used to take can now kill you. If you are going to use, use far less, never alone, and have naloxone on hand. In Australia it is free from pharmacies without a prescription.

The next 24 hours

  1. Stop now. A slip is one night. A relapse is deciding the night means you may as well keep going.
  2. Tell one person today. Sponsor, mate, counsellor, your person. Shame grows in silence and shrinks fast when spoken.
  3. Get rid of what's left.
  4. Eat, shower, sleep. You're dealing with a hangover and a guilt spiral at once. Look after the body first.
  5. Go to a meeting or your group within a day, and say what happened. You'll get nods, not judgement. Most of the room has been there.

The thought that does the damage

"I've blown it. All those days are gone. I'm back to zero, so I might as well drink." Psychologists have a name for that reaction, the abstinence violation effect, and it turns far more slips into full relapses than the drink itself does.

It's also wrong. The days you had are not erased. Your brain kept healing through every one of them. The skills you built are still there. You are not back at the start. You're a person with experience who had a bad night.

Relapse rates for addiction are around 40 to 60 percent, about the same as for other long-term conditions such as high blood pressure and asthma when treatment lapses. In a national US study, people who had resolved an alcohol or drug problem had made a median of two serious attempts before it stuck.

McLellan et al., JAMA, 2000; Kelly et al., 2019. Sources

Then get curious

A few days later, when the shame has cooled, go back over it like an investigator. A slip always has a run-up.

  • What was going on in the week before? Sleep, stress, isolation?
  • What had you stopped doing that had been working?
  • When was the actual decision? It's usually hours before the drink.
  • What would you do differently at that moment?

Write the answers on your Crew Card. That's your plan for next time, written by the person who knows you best.

About the counter

Reset it or don't. Some people need the clean date. Others track total sober days and count the slip as one day off out of two hundred. What matters is that you're on the right side of it again today.
