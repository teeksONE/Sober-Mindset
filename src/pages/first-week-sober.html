Know your danger hours

For most drinkers it's roughly 5 to 8 p.m.: the end of work, the start of the evening, the hour your hand used to reach for the fridge. You don't need willpower for that window. You need a plan, made earlier in the day, so you're not deciding anything at 5.15.

  • Be somewhere else: gym, pool, a walk, someone's house, a meeting
  • Eat early. Hunger and craving feel almost identical
  • Have something in your hand: soda and lime, ginger beer, tea
  • Change the route home if it passes the bottle shop

Four things to do this week

  1. Tell one person. Out loud or by text. Secrets are where relapse lives.
  2. See a GP if you haven't. Bloods, sleep, mood. Start with a clear picture.
  3. Plan every evening through to day seven. Write it down. It can be boring.
  4. Book one thing for next week with other sober people in it. A meeting, a group, a class. That's Stage 3, and it's the stage that makes the difference.

What you might feel

Some people get a burst of energy and optimism around day five. In the rooms they call it the pink cloud. Enjoy it and don't trust it with big decisions. Others feel flat, teary or furious. Both are normal. Your brain's reward system has been leaned on hard and it's finding its level again.

Sleep is usually still poor. It improves over weeks, not days. There's a whole page on it when you're ready.

Day seven

Mark it. Not with a drink, obviously. Buy the good coffee, go for the swim, tell your person. A week is real. A lot of people never get one.
