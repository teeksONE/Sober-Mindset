Before you go

  • Look for a meeting marked open. Anyone can attend those. "Closed" meetings are for people who think they might have a problem, which also includes you.
  • You don't book, register or give a surname. You just turn up.
  • Arrive five minutes early. Someone will say hello. If you say "it's my first one", they'll look after you.

What happens

  1. Someone opens the meeting and reads a short introduction.
  2. Depending on the format, one person shares their story for a while, or people take turns speaking for a few minutes each.
  3. Nobody interrupts, gives advice or responds to what anyone else said. People talk about themselves.
  4. A basket goes round for the rent and the coffee. Putting nothing in is completely fine.
  5. It ends after an hour, usually on the dot.

What you have to do

Nothing. If the sharing comes around to you, say "I'm just listening today". Everyone in that room has said exactly those words. You don't have to call yourself an alcoholic or an addict. You don't have to believe anything.

You will hear the word God or "higher power". For many people that means a religious God. For plenty of others it means the group, nature, or simply "something bigger than my own worst thinking". Nobody will check.

What surprises people

  • The laughing. Rooms full of people who've been through the worst of it tend to be funny.
  • The range. Tradies, lawyers, students, grandparents. Addiction doesn't sort by postcode.
  • Hearing your own story from a stranger's mouth. It usually happens in the first two or three meetings, and it's the moment most people decide to come back.

Afterwards

Stay five minutes. That's where the real meeting happens. Someone will probably offer you a phone number. Take it. You're not signing up for anything by having a number in your phone.

If this one didn't fit, try a different one. If none of them fit after a fair go, that's fine too. The next page is the rest of the menu.

This week

One meeting. Sit at the back.

Pick one from the finders on the last page, in person or online, and go once. You're not committing to a programme. You're collecting information.
