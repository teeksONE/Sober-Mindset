If you're a parent

  • Fund the tasters, not the gear. Four intro sessions cost less than most Schoolies weeks. Hold off on equipment until they've committed.
  • Drive. Early starts and distant trailheads are where these plans die. Lifts are the most useful thing you can offer.
  • Ask about it and then listen. "What surprised you?" works better than "Did you enjoy it?"
  • Let them choose. The sport that sticks is theirs, not the one you'd have picked.
  • Don't make it a lecture about alcohol. The programme never needs to mention it. The 6 a.m. session does the talking.

If you're a school

The window is Term 4 of Year 12, before students scatter. A workable version:

  1. Introduce it in a wellbeing or careers session. Students take the Find Your Thing quiz in the room.
  2. Line up four local providers for discounted taster sessions: a surf school, a climbing gym, a martial arts academy, a club. Most will say yes to a group booking in their quiet season.
  3. Form crews of four to eight around shared quiz results, each with a staff or alumni contact.
  4. Hold a Send night in February where crews show what they did. Invite the next Year 12.

It sits comfortably alongside the Duke of Edinburgh's Award, whose physical recreation and adventurous journey sections ask for much the same thing.

If you're a club or coach

You are the programme. A school leaver who walks into your gym in December and is still there in March has changed the direction of their twenties.

  • Offer a free or cheap school-leavers' taster in November and December
  • Pair each newcomer with a member a few years older
  • Look at what your club does after training. If the social life is the bar, build one that isn't

Safety basics for any group

  • Use providers with instructors accredited by the sport's national body, and check their insurance
  • Written parental consent for anyone under 18
  • Follow your organisation's child-safety and excursion policies, including working-with-children checks for adults involved
  • Collect health information and emergency contacts before the first session
  • Beginners' sessions only. Nobody is pushed past what they agree to

The design draws on research into sensation seeking, on Iceland's community-wide prevention model and on trials of personality-targeted programmes in schools. The science page sets it out, including what isn't known. First Summer itself has not been formally evaluated.
