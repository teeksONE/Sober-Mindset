What the research found

For decades people argued about whether AA "really works". In 2020 the question got a serious answer. A Cochrane review, the most rigorous kind there is, pooled 27 studies and more than 10,000 people.

AA and structured twelve-step programmes led to higher rates of continuous abstinence than other established treatments such as cognitive behavioural therapy, and did as well as them on other measures. They also probably save substantial healthcare costs.

Kelly, Humphreys & Ferri, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 2020. Sources

The follow-up research on why is the interesting part. It mostly isn't the bits people worry about. The biggest effects come from ordinary things: swapping a drinking social circle for a sober one, building confidence that you can get through a hard night, and picking up ways of coping from people a few steps ahead.

In other words, the active ingredient is other people. That matters, because it means it can be found in more than one kind of room.

Why it can feel daunting

Walk into an average meeting at 20 and you may be the youngest person there by a couple of decades. People are counting sobriety in years you haven't been alive. Some of the language is from the 1930s. It's easy to decide in ten minutes that this isn't for you.

Researchers have looked at this. Young adults did better early on when the meetings they attended included more people around their own age. So your instinct isn't weakness. It's information.

In a study of young adults after treatment, going to meetings with a higher share of similar-aged members was linked to better outcomes in the early months. Over time, mixed-age meetings mattered more: the older members are where the long-term experience is.

Labbe et al., Drug and Alcohol Dependence, 2013. Sources

How to find one that fits

  • Try six before you decide. Every meeting has its own crowd and mood. One bad one tells you nothing.
  • Look for young people's meetings. Most cities have them. Search your local AA or NA site for "young people" or "YPAA".
  • Try online first if the door is the hard part. Camera off, mic off, just listen.
  • Go to a speaker meeting. One person tells their story. Nobody expects anything from you.
  • Take the fellowship, leave the rest. You don't have to agree with every line of the literature to get the benefit of the people.
