Who I am

I got sober on November 12, 2014. I was 29. I'd spent the decade before that convinced a life without drinking would be small, grey and over by nine o'clock.

I was wrong about every part of it. Sober, I've spent ski seasons in Whistler, jumped out of planes, hunted, travelled and started over more than once. None of it was on offer while I was drinking.

I got sober through AA and I'll always credit it. Fellowship, honesty, gratitude and one day at a time are in the bones of this site.

Why the site exists

Two reasons. First, meetings are the most accessible free community there is, and they work. But a room full of people with thirty years of sobriety can be a hard door to walk through at 19. Younger people need to see more than one way in.

Second, I think a lot of young people who end up in addiction had too much intensity and nowhere to put it. That's what The Crossroads is about, and it's why First Summer exists: a programme that gets school leavers into high-adrenaline sport before the party becomes the identity.

How the site uses science

  • The voice is personal. The claims are checked. Where a page says "the research shows", the source is listed on the science page.
  • Systematic reviews and large trials come first. Single studies are described as single studies.
  • Where the evidence is mixed or thin, the page says so.
  • If I've got something wrong, I want to know.

What this site is not

It isn't medical advice or treatment, and it isn't a crisis service. I'm not a doctor. If you're in trouble right now, go to Help now. If you drink heavily every day, read Stopping safely before you do anything else.

The site is not affiliated with Alcoholics Anonymous or any organisation it mentions.

Your privacy

The tools on this site store what you enter in your own browser. Nothing you type into a counter, check-in or quiz is sent to me or anyone else.
