1. "Sober life is boring"

This is the big one, and it has it backwards. Drinking is the same night on repeat: same bar, same stories, same hangover. Sober is where the variety is. The mountain at 6 a.m., the trip you remember, the skill you actually got good at. See what's possible.

2. "I'm too young to have a problem"

There's no minimum age. The earlier heavy drinking starts, the more likely it is to become dependence, and the best time to change course is before it has cost you a decade. I waited until 29. I'd have given a lot to do it at 22.

3. "You have to hit rock bottom first"

Rock bottom is wherever you stop digging. Nobody hands out extra credit for losing more first. Plenty of people stop because they can see where the road goes, not because they've arrived.

4. "It's AA or nothing"

Meetings work and they're free, and I owe mine a lot. They're also one option among several: practical skills groups, meditation-based groups, counselling, medication, young people's meetings, online rooms. Here's the full menu.

5. "I'll lose all my friends"

You'll lose drinking buddies. Friends turn out to be the ones still around when the only thing on offer is coffee. And you'll meet people through whatever you start doing instead.

6. "Forever is unbearable"

It is. So don't do forever. Do today. "One day at a time" sounds like a bumper sticker until you try it and discover it's a piece of engineering: nobody can stay sober for forty years, and anybody can stay sober until bedtime.

You are also less alone than it looks. National surveys in Australia, the UK and the US all show young adults drinking less than the generation before them, with more choosing not to drink at all. And in a large US survey, about one adult in eleven said they had resolved a significant alcohol or drug problem. Recovery is the usual outcome, not the exception.
