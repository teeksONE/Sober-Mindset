SMART Recovery

Practical and secular. A trained facilitator runs a group through tools from cognitive behavioural therapy: handling urges, managing thoughts, building motivation. No labels, no higher power. Online and in person.

Good if you want techniques, not stories.

SMART Recovery Australia
🪷

Recovery Dharma

Peer-led groups built on Buddhist practice. Each meeting includes a meditation and a discussion. You don't need to be Buddhist or to have meditated before.

Good if sitting quietly appeals more than talking.

recoverydharma.org
🛟

LifeRing

Secular peer support with a conversational format. People talk about their week and what's coming up, and the group responds. Mostly online outside North America.

Good if you want back-and-forth conversation.

lifering.org
💬

A counsellor or psychologist

One-to-one, private, and able to work on what's underneath: anxiety, trauma, ADHD, grief. In Australia a GP can write a mental health treatment plan that makes sessions cheaper through Medicare.

Good alongside any group. Often the missing piece.

💊

Medication

Hardly anyone knows this exists. Naltrexone and acamprosate are prescription medicines that reduce cravings or make drinking less rewarding. Trials show both modestly improve the odds. A GP can prescribe them.

Not a cheat. A tool, like any other.

🧑‍🤝‍🧑

Youth services

If you're 12 to 25 in Australia, headspace offers free or low-cost support for mental health and alcohol and drug use, in centres and online, from people who work with your age group all day.

Good if "adult" services feel like the wrong room.

headspace.org.au
📱

Online communities

Reddit's r/stopdrinking is large, kind and awake at 3 a.m. Hello Sunday Morning's Daybreak app is an Australian anonymous community for changing your drinking. In The Rooms hosts live online meetings of many kinds.

Good for nights, remote towns and first steps.

🧗

A crew that does something

A climbing gym, a run club, a surf club, a jiu-jitsu academy. Not treatment, but for a lot of young people it's the community that actually sticks, and it works beautifully alongside any of the above.

Good if you connect by doing, not talking.

Find your crew

A study that followed people in SMART Recovery, LifeRing, Women for Sobriety and twelve-step groups for a year found the alternatives worked about as well as twelve-step groups for people who got properly involved. How much you take part matters more than which one you pick.

Zemore et al., Journal of Substance Abuse Treatment, 2018. Medication: Jonas et al., JAMA, 2014. Sources

How to choose

Pick the one you'll actually go back to. That's the entire test. Try two different kinds in the same fortnight and notice which one you didn't have to drag yourself to. Or let the quiz narrow it down.
