You can make it much more likely that they do, and you can look after yourself while you wait. Here is what helps and what doesn't. ---

What helps

  • Stay in the relationship. The most powerful thing you can offer is being someone they can come back to when the wheels come off. Don't burn that bridge to make a point.
  • Be specific about what worries you. Not "you're throwing your life away". Try "you didn't come home Thursday and I was scared." Concrete beats moral.
  • Notice the good days. Say something when they're sober and it's going well. Make sober time warmer than drinking time.
  • Offer the outlets. Pay for the surf lesson. Drive them to the gym. Suggest the climb. Don't wait for them to ask.
  • Pick your moment. Never mid-argument and never while they're affected. The morning after, calmly, is when it lands.

What backfires

  • Lecturing. They've heard it. It reinforces the identity you're trying to shift.
  • Ambushes and ultimatums you won't follow through on.
  • Cleaning up every consequence. Calling in sick for them, paying the fine, covering the story. Kindness that hides the cost delays the day they see it.
  • Waiting for rock bottom. There's no evidence people need to lose everything first, and for some the bottom is fatal.

An approach called CRAFT teaches families exactly these skills: rewarding sober behaviour, letting natural consequences land, and inviting the person into treatment at the right moment. In a trial comparing three approaches, CRAFT got 64 percent of resistant drinkers into treatment, against 30 percent for a confrontational intervention and 13 percent for a referral to Al-Anon alone.

Miller, Meyers & Tonigan, Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology, 1999. Sources

If they're 16 to 20

At this age you still have real influence, even when it doesn't feel like it. Time spent together, knowing where they are and who with, and having something organised to be part of are among the strongest protections researchers have found.

That's the thinking behind First Summer, a 12-week programme for school leavers. There's a page for parents on how to back it without taking it over.

Support for you

Living alongside someone's addiction is exhausting and lonely, and you're allowed to need help regardless of what they do.

  • Family Drug Support Australia: 24-hour line for families, 1300 368 186, fds.org.au
  • Al-Anon Family Groups: meetings for families and friends of problem drinkers, al-anon.org.au
  • Counselling Online: free alcohol and drug counselling by chat for anyone affected, counsellingonline.org.au
  • Your GP: for your own sleep, stress and health, which have probably been running last

If you're worried about their safety tonight

If they're unresponsive, having a seizure, or talking about ending their life, call emergency services. Numbers are on the Help now page.
