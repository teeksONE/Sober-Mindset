This is the usual shape for alcohol. Yours will vary with how much and how long you drank. If you're in the group that needs a doctor first, this page is not a substitute.

Hours 6 to 12

Restless, a bit sweaty, on edge. Your hands might tremble. You'll want a drink mostly out of habit: it's the time of day you'd normally have one. Eat a proper meal now, while you still feel like it.

Hours 12 to 24

The first night. Sleep will probably be bad. That's normal and it isn't a sign you're doing it wrong. Don't lie there fighting it. Get up, have a shower, put on something dull.

Hours 24 to 48

For most people this is the peak. Anxiety, headache, no appetite, a heart that feels too fast, a short fuse. Your brain is recalibrating and it's noisy about it. Every hour from here is one you never have to do again.

Hours 48 to 72

It starts to ease. You'll sleep a little. Food tastes like something. Somewhere on day three most people notice a moment of clear-headedness they haven't had in a long time. Hold on to that. It's a preview.

Not normal

Getting worse after day two instead of better. Confusion. Seeing things. A seizure. Any of those, call emergency services.

What actually helps

  • Water and food. Small and often. Toast, bananas, soup, eggs. Sugar cravings are normal; give in to them this week.
  • One person who knows. Text them morning and night. "Day 2, rough, still here" is enough.
  • An empty house. Pour it out or give it away before you start. Don't test yourself.
  • A cleared diary. Cancel anything optional. You've got one job.
  • Walking. Twenty minutes outside takes the edge off more than you'd believe.
  • Lower standards. Screens, baths, bad television. Whatever gets you to bedtime.

When a craving hits, open Urge Surf and let the timer run. It will pass whether you drink or not. You only have to find that out once to believe it.
