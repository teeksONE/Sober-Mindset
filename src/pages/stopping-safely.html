Emergency

A seizure, confusion, seeing or hearing things that aren't there, a high fever or a racing heart after stopping drinking is a medical emergency. Call 000 in Australia (911 in the US and Canada, 111 in New Zealand, 999 in the UK). Don't wait to see if it passes.

Why alcohol is different

Alcohol slows the brain down. Drink heavily for long enough and the brain compensates by running itself faster. Take the alcohol away overnight and it's still running fast with nothing holding it back. That's withdrawal: shakes, sweats, anxiety, no sleep. In a small number of heavy drinkers it goes further, into seizures or delirium tremens, and those can kill.

The same applies to benzodiazepines such as Valium and Xanax. Never stop those suddenly.

See a doctor before you stop if

  • You drink every day, or most days, and have for weeks or longer
  • You get the shakes, sweats or nausea in the morning, or need a drink to steady yourself
  • You've had a seizure or severe withdrawal before
  • You're also taking benzodiazepines, opioids or other sedatives
  • You're pregnant, or you have a heart, liver or serious mental health condition

If none of those is you, stopping on your own is generally safe. Unpleasant, maybe. But safe. If you're unsure, ask. One appointment costs you nothing.

What "medical help" actually looks like

Much less dramatic than it sounds. You tell a GP how much you really drink. They've heard bigger numbers. For most people the answer is a home detox: a short course of medication over about a week, a few check-ins, someone staying with you. People at higher risk go to a detox unit or hospital for a few days.

In Australia, the National Alcohol and Other Drug Hotline (1800 250 015) is free, confidential and will tell you what's available near you.

Other substances

  • Opioids. Withdrawal is miserable but rarely dangerous in itself. The danger comes after: your tolerance drops fast, so the dose you used to take can kill you if you go back to it. Ask a doctor about treatment options; they work.
  • Stimulants (meth, cocaine). Expect a crash: exhaustion, hunger, very low mood. If the low turns into thoughts of ending things, that's a Help now moment.
  • Cannabis. Irritability, poor sleep and vivid dreams for a week or two. Uncomfortable, not dangerous.

Alcohol withdrawal symptoms usually begin 6 to 24 hours after the last drink and peak between 24 and 72 hours. Seizures are most likely in the first two days. Delirium tremens, the most severe form, typically appears two to four days in and affects only a small minority of people in withdrawal, but it needs hospital care.
