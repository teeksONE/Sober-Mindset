# September

## 09-01 · A professional ear
status: approved

Some things are too tangled, or too private, to sort out with friends. A counsellor or psychologist offers a room where you can say anything, to someone trained to help and bound to keep it confidential. They are on nobody's side but yours, and they have no history with you. What sits underneath drinking is often old: grief, fear, things that happened long ago. A professional can help you look at it safely and at your own pace. Seeing one is ordinary maintenance, the same as seeing a mechanic or a dentist. If cost is a worry, ask a GP what is available. Many services are free or subsidised.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that skilled help exists, and that I am allowed to use it.

## 09-02 · Small steps into fear
status: approved

A feared thing looks huge from a distance. The phone call, the appointment, the first sober wedding, the conversation with your father. Seen whole, it can stop you cold. Cut it into pieces. What is the smallest first step? Find the number. Write the first line. Drive past the venue. Do only that, and then stop if you want to. Each small step shows you that nothing terrible happened, and the next one gets easier. By the time you reach the thing itself, you have already done most of it. Fear shrinks when you walk toward it slowly, a piece at a time.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for one small step I took toward something that scares me.

**Affirmations:** 134, 44, 176

## 09-03 · New traditions
status: approved

Many traditions have a drink at the centre. The toast, the first beer of the holiday, the bottle opened on a birthday. Take the drink away and the tradition can feel hollow. So start your own. It can be anything, as long as you repeat it. A swim on the morning of your birthday. Pancakes on the first day of every holiday. A walk to the same lookout each New Year. A particular meal to mark good news. Invite people in. After two or three rounds, it stops being new and becomes simply what you do. Traditions are only habits that people have grown fond of.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the customs I get to create.

**Affirmations:** 346, 274, 109

## 09-04 · A wandering line
status: approved

Progress rarely travels in a straight line. It wanders. You have a strong fortnight and then a shaky few days. You handle a big event well and then come undone over something small. Looked at day by day, this can seem like going nowhere. Stand further back and the direction is clear. You are sleeping better than you were. You recover from bad moments faster. You know more about yourself. A doubling back is part of the route. If there has been a slip on the way, that is a bend in the line as well, and the line continues. Measure across months, and be gentle with single days.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the direction I am heading in, bends and all.

**Affirmations:** 245, 308, 358

## 09-05 · Feelings pass
status: approved

Think of the worst feeling you ever had. The panic, the shame, the heartbreak that seemed endless. It ended. Every feeling you have ever had has ended, including the ones you were sure would last. That is worth remembering in the middle of a hard hour, because strong feelings always claim to be permanent. They rise, hold for a while and thin out, usually faster than you expect. You can help by staying still, breathing and letting the feeling be there without feeding it a story. Good feelings pass as well, which is a reason to notice them while they are here.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that no hard feeling has ever stayed forever.

**Affirmations:** 142, 269, 2

## 09-06 · The person ahead
status: approved

Picture yourself a year from now, doing well. Where are you living? What does your morning look like? How do you carry yourself? Make the picture as detailed as you can. That person is real in the sense that they are being built now, out of what you do each day. They are relying on you. Ask what they would thank you for today. It might be an early night, a difficult phone call, twenty minutes of exercise or simply staying sober until bedtime. Do that one thing as a gift to them. Tomorrow, do another. You and that person get closer with every day you spend this way.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for who I am becoming, little by little.

**Affirmations:** 244, 314, 273

## 09-07 · Pulling away
status: approved

Pulling away from people is among the earliest signs of trouble. You let calls go to voicemail. You skip the group, the game, the usual coffee. You tell yourself you are tired or busy. Sometimes that is true. Often something is brewing, and being alone lets it grow unobserved. Isolation feels like protection, and it usually works against you. When you notice yourself withdrawing, do one small thing in the other direction. Answer one message. Turn up for half an hour. Tell one person that you have been hiding a bit. The reasons can wait. Being seen is enough to break the spell.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the people I can turn back toward.

**Affirmations:** 82, 152, 114

## 09-08 · Shared effort
status: approved

Do something hard with other people. Paddle in a crew. Join a working bee. Train for an event with a group. Carry a mate's furniture up three flights of stairs. Shared effort bonds people faster than any amount of talk. You suffer a little together, you rely on each other, and at the end there is a result that belongs to all of you. Nobody has to share feelings or make conversation. The work does the introducing. Friendships made this way tend to be sturdy, because they rest on something done. Look for a team, a crew or a project that needs hands, and offer yours.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for anyone I have worked hard alongside.

**Affirmations:** 289, 212, 76

## 09-09 · Uneven energy
status: approved

Your energy may be all over the place for a time. One day you clean the whole house and go for a run. The next you can barely get off the couch. This is common, and it can go on for some months. Your body is recalibrating after years of being pushed and sedated in turn. Try to go with it. On the good days, do a bit less than you could, so you don't borrow from tomorrow. On the flat days, do a small amount anyway and call it enough. The swings tend to even out. Meanwhile, neither kind of day is the truth about you.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for whatever energy I have today, large or small.

**Affirmations:** 104, 360, 215

## 09-10 · Good company
status: approved

A common fear is that without a drink you will be dull, and people will drift off. Then something surprising happens. A friend says they like you better this way. Someone seeks you out at a party because you are the one making sense. You get invited again. It turns out that people enjoy being listened to, remembered and laughed with, and you are better at all three. You are also there at the end of the night, able to drive. If you doubt that you are good company, look at the evidence. Who rings you? Who is glad when you walk in? Believe them.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that people enjoy having me around as I am.

**Affirmations:** 230, 33, 97

## 09-11 · Volunteering
status: approved

Somewhere near you, a group needs another pair of hands on a regular day. A food bank, a sports club, an animal shelter, a community garden, a surf club, a charity shop. Volunteering gives you a place to be and people who expect you. It gets you out of your own head for a few hours. It also changes how you see yourself. For a time you may have felt like someone who took more than they gave. Being needed, on a schedule, turns that around. Start with a small commitment you can keep, such as two hours a fortnight. Turn up every time.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for being useful to someone.

**Affirmations:** 324, 250, 20

## 09-12 · Regret
status: approved

Regret is the price of having a conscience and a memory. You look back and wish you had done it differently. In small amounts it is useful. It shows you what you value and what you won't do again. In large amounts it takes over. You spend today rerunning yesterday and have nothing left for now. Let regret inform you, and decline to let it run the day. When it comes up, ask whether there is anything in it you can act on. If there is, do it or plan it. If there isn't, say so to yourself, and bring your attention back to what is in front of you.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for what my regrets have taught me, and for a new day to use it.

**Affirmations:** 144, 38, 310

## 09-13 · The comparison trap
status: approved

Someone, somewhere, always drinks more than you do. The mate who never goes home. The uncle. The bloke at the bar every afternoon. As long as you measure yourself against them, you can decide you are fine. That comparison keeps the real question closed. The useful measure is your own life. Is drinking costing you sleep, money, mornings, trust? Is it getting in the way of what you want? Other people's habits can't answer that. If you are long sober, the same trap works in reverse: comparing your progress with someone else's. Either way, look at your own page.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for being able to look honestly at my own life.

**Affirmations:** 5, 49, 99

## 09-14 · Anxiety
status: approved

Anxiety lives in the future. It is busy with what might happen tomorrow, next month, in five years. Your body responds as if the danger were in the room: tight chest, fast pulse, shallow breath. The quickest way back is through your senses. Press your feet into the floor. Name five things you can see and three you can hear. Hold something cold. Breathe out for longer than you breathe in. For a moment you are here, where nothing is attacking you. The worry may return, and you can do it again. If anxiety is constant or overwhelming, that is worth taking to a professional.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for this moment, where I am safe.

**Affirmations:** 128, 105, 252

## 09-15 · Helping without rescuing
status: approved

When someone you care about is struggling, it is natural to want to carry them. You make their calls, pay their bills, cover for them, lie awake planning their recovery. It comes from love, and it tends to wear you out while changing little. Their choices are theirs. You can offer information, company, a lift to an appointment and an honest opinion. You can say what you will and won't do. The wanting has to come from them. Stepping back from rescuing is hard, and it can be mistaken for coldness, when it is a form of respect. It also keeps you well enough to be there when they are ready.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can care about someone without carrying them.

**Affirmations:** 328, 247, 130

## 09-16 · The first one
status: approved

You never have to refuse the tenth drink. Only the first. That makes the job much smaller than it looks. All the trouble is on the far side of that one decision, and none of it can reach you while the first drink stays on the bar. When your mind starts running ahead to a whole night or a whole life, bring it back to this. There is a single glass in question, and a single answer. Say no to that, and everything after it is taken care of. People sober for decades are still doing exactly this. One drink, declined, as often as it comes up.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that there is only ever one drink to say no to.

**Affirmations:** 47, 24, 77

## 09-17 · Not alone
status: approved

Nobody gets sober alone, and nobody stays sober alone. Other people are the strongest support there is. They notice what you miss. They have been where you are. They remind you who you are on days you forget. You may be proud of managing by yourself, and independence is a fine quality. It has limits here. A problem that grew in private usually needs company to shrink. Support can look however you like: a group, a counsellor, a team, two good friends, an online forum. What matters is that someone knows how you are really going. If you have people already, tell one of them today that they matter.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I don't have to do this by myself.

**Affirmations:** 61, 254, 161

## 09-18 · What it takes
status: approved

Make a plain list of what drinking or using takes from you. Leave judgement out and stick to facts. Hours of sleep. Mornings. Money, by the week. Days of feeling unwell. Things said that had to be taken back. Trust, with particular people. Fitness. Plans that never happened. Write it all down and look at it. Seeing the costs in one place is different from knowing them one at a time. If you stopped a while ago, write the list from memory and put it beside what you have now. Read the page as an invoice, and decide whether you want to keep paying it.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for seeing the real cost clearly.

**Affirmations:** 6, 235, 353

## 09-19 · Mistakes at work
status: approved

You will make a mistake at work. Everyone does. The wrong figure, the missed deadline, the email to the wrong person. In the drinking days a mistake might have been one of many, hidden or blamed on something else. Sober, it stands out, and you may feel a rush of shame far bigger than the error deserves. Keep it in proportion. Tell whoever needs to know, promptly and plainly. Say what happened and what you will do to fix it. Then fix it. Most bosses respect someone who owns an error far more than someone who never admits one. Afterwards, let it go. One mistake is one mistake.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can get something wrong and put it right.

**Affirmations:** 193, 172, 64

## 09-20 · The word forever
status: approved

Forever is a frightening word. Never again, for the rest of your life, at every wedding and every funeral. Put like that, it is too heavy for anyone to pick up, and the weight alone can send people back. Nobody is asking you to lift forever. Nobody can. Life only arrives in single days, so that is the only size you ever have to manage. Can you stay sober until tonight? That is the whole question. People with thirty years did it in exactly those pieces. When forever starts looming, shrink the frame. Today is the right size.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that today is the only day I have to manage.

**Affirmations:** 18, 197, 286

## 09-21 · Speaking
status: approved

Say one true thing aloud today to someone who will understand it. It might be in a group, to a counsellor or across a kitchen table. That you are finding this hard. That you nearly drank last week. That you are happier than you expected and don't trust it. Thoughts that go round silently in your head get heavier and stranger. Spoken, they become ordinary, and other people often nod. Your voice may shake the first time. You may feel exposed afterwards for an hour. Then comes the relief. Speaking is how the inside and the outside of you get back in line, and that is a steadier way to live.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for having a voice, and somewhere to use it.

**Affirmations:** 67, 326, 362

## 09-22 · Taking stock
status: approved

Every so often, stop and take stock. Look back over the last few weeks and ask what has shifted. It might be something clear, such as a number of sober days. It might be subtle. You are less jumpy. You laughed more. A difficult person bothers you less. You handled something that would once have flattened you. Changes like these are hard to see while they are happening, and easy to see once you look back. Note what got harder as well, without alarm. That tells you where to put some attention next. Write three lines about where you are. Read them the next time you take stock.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for what has changed since the last time I stopped to look.

**Affirmations:** 31, 292, 79

## 09-23 · A clear head at work
status: approved

Work is easier with a clear head. You arrive without having to hide how you feel. You follow what is said in the meeting. The middle of the afternoon is no longer lost to a headache, or spent counting the hours until you can leave. Problems that used to seem enormous turn out to be manageable when you are rested. People may notice before you do: sharper, steadier, more patient. If your work suffered during the drinking, this is where it starts to recover. If it never seemed to suffer, you may be surprised by how much effort the hiding took. Enjoy the plain satisfaction of a job done properly.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for bringing my whole mind to what I do.

**Affirmations:** 189, 222, 140

## 09-24 · Repair
status: approved

Some harm can be mended. Money can be repaid. A broken thing can be replaced. A lie can be corrected. A neglected friendship can be tended. Repair begins with asking whether it is wanted. Some people will welcome it. Others would prefer to be left alone, and contacting them would serve you more than them. Think it through, ideally with someone wise. Where repair is possible and welcome, be practical and specific. Offer what you can and carry it out. Where it is impossible, you can still make a kind of repair elsewhere, by doing good for someone who needs it. What matters is that the mending is real.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for every chance to mend something.

**Affirmations:** 313, 301, 160

## 09-25 · Learning
status: approved

Is there something you always meant to learn? A language, an instrument, a trade skill, how engines work, how to swim properly. It may have sat on the list for years while evenings went elsewhere. A clear head learns well. You take in more, and you still have it the next day. Sign up for a class, borrow a book, find a course online or ask someone to show you. Twenty minutes a day is enough to make real progress in a year. Learning also gives you a new answer to the question of what you have been up to. Pick the thing. Take the first lesson soon.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for a mind that can still learn new things.

**Affirmations:** 165, 298, 306

## 09-26 · A daily practice
status: approved

At the end of each day, find three good things. They can be very small. A decent coffee. A message from a friend. A green light when you were running late. Write them down or say them aloud. For the first week it may feel forced, and some days you will struggle to reach three. Keep going. What you are doing is training your attention. The mind is quick to spot what went wrong and slow to register what went right, and this evens the balance. After a while you start noticing good things as they happen, because part of you is collecting them for tonight.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for three good things in this day, however small.

**Affirmations:** 318, 168, 87

## 09-27 · Driving
status: approved

You can drive. At any hour, for any reason, without doing sums in your head about how many you have had. It is easy to overlook how large a freedom that is. You can leave when you want to. You can take a sick friend to hospital at midnight. You can collect the kids, give a lift to a stranded mate, or go to the coast before dawn on a whim. You are never the risk on the road. If you have lost your licence, the day you get it back will mean a great deal. Meanwhile, every sober day is a day you would be safe behind the wheel.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for being safe to drive, and for the freedom that brings.

**Affirmations:** 199, 141, 261

## 09-28 · Where it sits
status: approved

Next time an urge comes, look for it in your body. Where does it sit? It might be a tightness in the chest, a clench in the jaw, a hollow in the stomach, a restlessness in the hands. Give it your full attention, as if you were describing it to a doctor. How big is the area? Is it hot or cold, still or moving? As you watch, it will change. It shifts, swells or fades. An urge that seemed like a command turns out to be a set of sensations, and sensations can be observed. You are bigger than the feeling, and you can hold it without obeying it.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can watch an urge without having to follow it.

**Affirmations:** 35, 277, 1

## 09-29 · Lying awake
status: approved

You are awake, and it is the middle of the night. The clock says three. Your mind is running through everything that could go wrong. In the drinking days a few drinks would have knocked you out, badly. Now you are simply awake. A sleepless night is unpleasant and survivable. Tomorrow will be slower, and you will get through it. Nothing is undone by one bad night. If you can't sleep after a while, get up. Sit somewhere dim and read something dull. Go back to bed when your eyes are heavy. Try to leave the sums about how many hours are left. Rest still counts.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for a bed to rest in, even on the nights sleep is slow to come.

**Affirmations:** 95, 166, 54

## 09-30 · Wanting closeness
status: approved

Sometimes what feels like wanting a drink is wanting company. A lot of drinking happened with other people, or in places where people were. The glass came with talk, warmth and a sense of being part of something. When you are lonely, your mind may reach for the drink because that is the route it knows. Ask what you are really after. If it is closeness, a drink won't supply it. Ring someone. Go where people are: a café, a class, a late-opening pool. Sit with the family instead of in another room. Cook for somebody. The need is a good one, and there are better ways to meet it.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the people I can reach out to when I need company.

**Affirmations:** 45, 70, 232
