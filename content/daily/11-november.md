# November

## 11-01 · Reasons of your own
status: approved

People stop for all sorts of reasons. A partner's ultimatum. A doctor's warning. A boss's last chance. Those can get you started, and they are worth respecting. Over time, a reason borrowed from someone else tends to wear thin. If you are sober for them, you may resent them on a bad day. Look for reasons that are yours. What do you want from this? Clear mornings, a body that works, self-respect, the chance to do something you care about. Your reasons may be different at the start and years later. Check them now and then. When the reason belongs to you, nobody can take it away.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for my own reasons, whatever they are today.

**Affirmations:** 21, 47, 113

## 11-02 · Forgiving yourself
status: approved

Forgiving yourself is rarely done in one go. You decide to let something rest, and a little later it is back, as sharp as before. Forgiveness is a practice, and it works by repetition. Each time the memory returns, you acknowledge it, take whatever responsibility is yours, and put it down again. Slowly it loses its heat. It helps to remember the state you were in then, and what you knew. You were doing a poor job with poor tools. You have better ones now. Forgiving yourself lets nobody off the hook. It frees your hands for repair and for today.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can put the past down again each time it comes back.

**Affirmations:** 145, 177, 225

## 11-03 · Honest numbers
status: approved

Try counting, privately, with no verdict attached. How many drinks on a typical night? How many nights in a fortnight? How much money in a month? Use real numbers, measured and written down. Most people guess low. You are only gathering facts, the way you would read a fuel gauge. Nobody else has to see them. If the numbers are fine, you have lost nothing. If they surprise you, you know something true that you didn't know before, and you can decide what to do with it. If you stopped long ago, the same honest count works for sleep, spending, screens or anything else.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the truth about my own habits.

**Affirmations:** 3, 293, 360

## 11-04 · Outlasting it
status: approved

Some stretches can't be fixed, solved or improved. They can only be outlasted. A long illness in the family. A slow legal matter. A long grief. Months of money trouble. You do the right things and the situation stays hard. Endurance is the whole task, and it is an underrated skill. Get up, do the day, go to bed sober, repeat. Lower your expectations for everything else. Take help when it is offered. Somewhere in the middle it feels as though it will never end. Hard stretches do end. One morning you notice the weight is less. Having outlasted one, you know you can outlast another.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for my own endurance.

**Affirmations:** 273, 19, 91

## 11-05 · Counting days
status: approved

Counting sober days helps some people a great deal. The number grows, and you don't want to lose it. Milestones give you something to mark. For other people, or at other times, the count becomes a burden. It turns into a score to defend, and a slip feels like losing everything. The number is a tool. Pick it up when it encourages you and put it down when it starts to weigh on you. Some count from one date. Some count total sober days. Some stop counting after a year and just live. Whichever you choose, the days themselves are real whether or not anyone is tallying them.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for today's sober day, counted or not.

**Affirmations:** 24, 327, 345

## 11-06 · Parents
status: approved

A time comes when you start to see your parents as people. They were young once, and frightened, and working with whatever they had been given. They made mistakes, some of them serious. They may have drunk too, or been hard, or been absent. You can see them as people and still name what hurt. Understanding how it happened can loosen its hold. If your parents are alive, you might ask about their lives before you. If they have died, you can still wonder. And if contact with them is unsafe, you can keep your distance and still put some of the weight down.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for whatever good came to me through my parents.

**Affirmations:** 322, 237, 303

## 11-07 · Young and sober
status: approved

Being young and sober can feel like standing still while the whole crowd moves the other way. Your friends are at the pub, at festivals, on trips built around drinking. You may be the only person you know who doesn't. That is lonely some nights. It also gives you a great deal. You will remember your twenties. You have money, mornings and a clear head while others are spending all three. More people your age are choosing this than you might think, and they are findable, online and in person. If you are older now, think of the young person who needs to see that it can be done.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the years I get to be fully awake for.

**Affirmations:** 222, 6, 137

## 11-08 · Thanks on a hard day
status: approved

On a day when everything has gone wrong, look for one thing to be glad of. Only one. It may take some searching. The tea was hot. The bus came. A stranger held the door. You are still sober. The day can still be a bad one, and you can say so. Finding one good thing in it keeps the bad from being the whole story. It is a small act of defiance. Done regularly, it changes what you notice. Before you sleep tonight, name your one thing, however small it is. Some days it will be the only good thing, and it will be enough.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for one good thing in a hard day.

**Affirmations:** 331, 231, 267

## 11-09 · When someone else goes back
status: approved

Eventually someone you know goes back to drinking. It might be a friend from a group, or a person you looked up to. It can shake you badly. If they couldn't do it, with all their time, what chance do you have? Their relapse is about their life: their stresses, their supports, their choices in a hard hour. It says nothing about yours. Be kind to them. Leave the door open, because many people return. Then look to your own basics, and talk to someone about how it has affected you. You can care about them and stay on your own road.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for my own sober day, and I wish the same for those who are struggling.

**Affirmations:** 260, 104, 179

## 11-10 · Staying teachable
status: approved

The moment you decide you know everything about staying sober is a risky one. There is always a better way to handle a craving, a mood or a relationship, and someone else may have found it. Stay open to being shown. Listen to newcomers as well as old hands. Read something outside your usual range. When a friend suggests you are being stubborn, consider that they may be right. Being teachable takes some humility, and it pays for itself. You keep improving, where a closed mind stays where it is. Ask one person soon how they deal with something you find hard.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can still learn from other people.

**Affirmations:** 228, 12, 363

## 11-11 · Naming a feeling
status: approved

A feeling with a name is easier to carry. Unnamed, it is a vague heaviness or a jittery fog, and you don't know what to do about it. Stop and look for the word. Is it sadness, or disappointment? Is it anger, or hurt? Is it fear, or just tiredness? The more exact the word, the better. Once you can say that you are feeling lonely, or ashamed, or overlooked, the feeling shrinks to a size you can hold, and the next step is often obvious. If words are hard to find, look up a list of feelings and choose from it. This is a skill, and it improves with use.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for finding the right word for how I feel.

**Affirmations:** 123, 105, 56

## 11-12 · Saving for something
status: approved

Pick something you want, and start putting money toward it. Make it specific enough to picture. A surfboard. A trip to a particular place. A course. A deposit. A decent mattress. Work out the cost and open a separate account or get a jar. Each payday, move across some of what you would once have spent on drinking. Watching the total climb is a pleasure of its own, and it turns sober days into something you can see. When cravings come, the jar is an answer. When you reach the amount, buy the thing without guilt. Then choose the next one.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for something to save for, and for watching it grow.

**Affirmations:** 187, 142, 350

## 11-13 · Grief
status: approved

Grief keeps to no schedule. You can grieve people who died, and people who are alive and gone from your life. You can grieve time, health, chances that won't come again. Sober, you feel it without a buffer, and that is hard. It comes in waves, with calm stretches between. Let it come when it comes. Cry, talk, write, walk. Give it some of each day and then turn to something else for a while. There is no correct length and no finish line that everyone reaches. It changes shape as you carry it. If it stops you functioning, find a counsellor who knows grief.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for what I loved enough to grieve.

**Affirmations:** 129, 55, 67

## 11-14 · Invitations
status: approved

An invitation is a question, and it has two possible answers. You can say no to any of them. The engagement party at a pub, the boozy lunch, the reunion. A short, warm reply does it: thank you, you can't make it, you hope it goes well. No reason is required. You can also say yes, on your own terms, to the ones you want. Yes to the ceremony and no to the reception. Yes for an hour. Yes if you can drive. Decide by asking what the event will cost you and what it will give you. The people who matter will still be there next time.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the freedom to say yes or no.

**Affirmations:** 336, 320, 172

## 11-15 · Day one
status: approved

Every long stretch of sobriety began with a single ordinary day. Nothing marked it out at the time. The person simply didn't drink, and went to bed. If today is that day for you, it probably doesn't feel like much. It may feel awful. That is how it starts for nearly everyone. You are doing the hardest part right now, with the least to show for it. If your first day was long ago, try to remember it. Who helped? What got you through to the evening? Then think of the person somewhere near you who is on theirs today, and what you could offer them.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the day it started, and for anyone starting today.

**Affirmations:** 9, 318, 66

## 11-16 · Being there
status: approved

Some days matter a great deal to other people. The school concert. The graduation. The wedding. The final. The hospital visit. For a time you may have missed them, or been there in body only. Now you can turn up properly. You arrive on time. You watch the whole thing. You remember what was said, and you are fit to drive home afterwards. To the person whose day it is, your being there, clear-eyed, may mean more than any present. The ones you missed are gone, and the next one is still ahead. Put the dates in your calendar and treat them as fixed.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can be present for the people I love.

**Affirmations:** 326, 125, 63

## 11-17 · Overwork
status: approved

When drinking stops, work can quietly take its place. You stay late. You check email at midnight. You take on more because you finally have the energy, and because being busy keeps the feelings away. It looks respectable, and people praise you for it. Underneath, it is doing the same job the drink did. Watch for the signs: no time off, no hobbies, irritability when you are away from work, exhaustion you push through. Set an end to the working day and keep it. Take your days off. Work is a good thing in its place. A life needs more than one thing holding it up.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for my work, and for the life I have outside it.

**Affirmations:** 194, 151, 339

## 11-18 · Order
status: approved

Make your bed. Clear the bench. Put yesterday's clothes away. These take five minutes, and they change how a room feels. A tidy room has a steadying effect on a busy head. It gives your mind fewer things to snag on, and it is quietly encouraging to walk into. Nobody becomes a neat person overnight. Pick one surface and keep it clear. Do the dishes before bed, so the morning starts clean. When life feels chaotic, a small patch of order is something you can control. It also tells you, each time you see it, that someone is looking after this place.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for one clear, tidy space.

**Affirmations:** 167, 284, 219

## 11-19 · Home
status: approved

The place you live can help you or wear you down. If it is dark, cluttered and full of reminders, you won't want to be there, and you will look for somewhere else to spend your evenings. Make it somewhere you like coming back to. This needn't cost much. Open the windows. Wash the sheets. Move the furniture so the room feels new. Put up a photo of a place you love. Get a plant and keep it alive. Find one comfortable chair and a good lamp. If you share a house, start with your own room. A home that feels like yours makes a quiet night much easier.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for a roof over my head and a corner that is mine.

**Affirmations:** 200, 83, 31

## 11-20 · Your own way home
status: approved

Wherever you go, have your own way out. Drive yourself. Keep enough money for a taxi. Know the last bus. If you came with someone, agree beforehand that you might leave separately. Depending on another person for a lift means waiting until they are ready, and they may be ready three hours and six drinks after you. That wait is where a lot of trouble starts. With your own transport, leaving is a decision you can make in ten seconds. You may never use it. Knowing that you could go at any moment often makes it easier to stay and enjoy yourself.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can always get myself home.

**Affirmations:** 340, 170, 111

## 11-21 · Skills that transfer
status: approved

Staying sober teaches you things that work everywhere else. You learn to sit with discomfort without acting on it. You learn to break a big thing into days. You learn to ask for help, tell the truth, start again after a setback and keep a commitment when you don't feel like it. These are the same skills that build a career, a fitness habit, a relationship or a business. You have been training them under hard conditions. Next time you face a difficult project, remember what you already know how to do. If you can get through a craving, you can get through a deadline.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for what staying sober has taught me about everything else.

**Affirmations:** 212, 314, 281

## 11-22 · The list
status: approved

Write a list of things you want to do while you are able to. Big things and small ones. Swim with a turtle. See snow. Learn to make pasta. Run ten kilometres. Take your mother to the place she grew up. Camp alone for a night. Leave realism out of it for now. Just write. Then put the list somewhere you will see it, and choose one item to start on. For years, plans like these stayed plans. Sober, you have the mornings, the money and the memory to carry them out. A list also gives you something to turn toward on flat days. Add to it whenever a new idea arrives.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for everything I still want to do.

## 11-23 · Going back
status: approved

One visit to a group tells you very little. You are nervous, you know nobody, and you spend most of it working out where to sit. Go back. The second time, a face is familiar. The third time, someone uses your name. By the sixth, you have a seat you tend to take and a person you look for. Community is built from repeat visits, and there is no shortcut. This is true of a support group, a sports club, a class or a café. If you have a place like that already, keep turning up, especially when you don't feel like it. Your being there is part of what makes it a community for everyone else.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for a place where I am becoming a regular.

**Affirmations:** 65, 34, 126

## 11-24 · What a craving is
status: approved

A craving is your brain predicting a reward. Over years it learned that certain cues were followed by a drink, and it now fires up in advance whenever one appears. That surge of wanting is the prediction. It feels urgent, like hunger or thirst. It is closer to a notification on a phone: a signal that can be left unanswered. Nothing bad happens if you let it ring. The feeling peaks and fades, and each time the prediction goes unmet, it weakens slightly. Knowing this won't make a craving pleasant, but it makes it less mysterious and less personal. Your brain is running an old program.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for understanding what a craving is, and that I can let it ring.

**Affirmations:** 32, 139, 223

## 11-25 · Free days
status: approved

A whole day with nothing in it sounds like a luxury. For someone staying sober it can be a hazard. The hours spread out, and by mid-afternoon you are restless and looking for something to happen. Give the day a loose shape before it starts. One thing in the morning that gets you out of the house. One thing in the afternoon. One person to see or ring. A meal you will cook. Leave plenty of slack between them. This is far looser than a timetable. A few fixed points are enough to keep the day from drifting. Then enjoy the rest of it. A free day with a little structure tends to end well.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for a free day and a few good things to put in it.

**Affirmations:** 159, 191, 82

## 11-26 · Dreams about using
status: approved

You wake with your heart pounding, sure that you drank. It takes a few seconds to realise it was a dream. Then comes relief, and sometimes guilt or fear. Drinking dreams are very common, at the start and years later. They tend to come in clusters, often during stress or change. They say nothing about your commitment and they predict nothing. Your sleeping brain is sorting old material. Some people take the relief on waking as a useful reminder of how much their sobriety matters to them. If a dream leaves you rattled, tell someone that morning. You didn't drink, and the day ahead is still yours.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for waking up and finding that I am still sober.

**Affirmations:** 50, 209, 328

## 11-27 · Close to home
status: approved

An adventure needs no plane ticket. Look at a map of the area within an hour of your front door. There will be places you have never been: a creek, a ridge, a small town, a lookout, a strange little museum, a beach on the other side of the headland. Pick one and go soon. Take lunch. Walk somewhere without knowing what is at the end. Adventure is mostly a matter of going somewhere new with your eyes open, and that can be done on a free afternoon. Regular small trips do more for your mood than one big holiday a year. Your own district is bigger than you think.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the unexplored places close to where I live.

## 11-28 · Receiving kindness
status: approved

When someone does something kind for you, the reflex may be to refuse it or to start working out how to pay it back. You insist it is too much. You keep a mental tally. Being on the receiving end is uncomfortable if you have felt like a burden. Try simply accepting. Say thank you and let it land. The person offering is getting something too, because helping feels good. Refusing takes that from them. You will have plenty of chances to give later, to them or to someone else. For now, let yourself be helped. It is part of how people stay connected.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for a kindness I received, and I let myself keep it.

## 11-29 · A big night
status: approved

You can still have a big night. A concert where you sing until you are hoarse. Dancing until your shirt is soaked. A final that goes to extra time. A wedding where you are the last one on the floor. The energy of a crowd and loud music is its own intoxicant, and you are allowed all of it. Eat beforehand. Keep a drink of your own in your hand. Stick with people who are good to be around. When it starts to turn sloppy, go. You get the wild part, you drive yourself home, and you wake up with the whole night intact. That is a better deal than the old one.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for big nights I get to remember.

## 11-30 · A crowded calendar
status: approved

A run of social occasions is coming. End-of-year drinks, family lunches, parties, public holidays. Taken one by one they are manageable. Stacked together they are a marathon. Get the whole calendar in front of you today. Decide which events you will attend, which you will skip and which you will leave early. Spread them out so there are quiet nights between. Book in the things that keep you steady, so they don't get squeezed out. Tell your support people that this stretch is coming. Stock the fridge with drinks you like. With a plan made now, you can walk into a busy month and enjoy the good parts of it.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for time to prepare, and for the people I will see.

**Affirmations:** 335, 202, 285
