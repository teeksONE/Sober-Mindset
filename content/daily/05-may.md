## 05-01 · Work drinks
status: approved

The round after work is one of the trickier rituals. It is where people relax, complain about the boss and bond. Skipping it every time can leave you on the outside. Going can feel risky. There is a middle path. Turn up for the first half hour, when people are still themselves. Order something without alcohol before anyone offers. Talk to two or three people, then say you have somewhere to be. Nobody keeps track of what is in your glass. You can also suggest other ways to get together: a lunch, a coffee run, a walk at the break. If the round is too much for now, leave it for later.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can be part of a team and still look after myself.

## 05-02 · Burning it off
status: approved

A craving lives in a certain state of the body: tight, restless, keyed up. Change the state and the craving often changes with it. The quickest way is a short burst of hard effort. Twenty squats. A sprint to the end of the street. Push-ups until your arms shake. Two minutes of anything that leaves you breathing hard. Afterwards your heart is thumping for a different reason, and the urge has usually lost its grip. This works in a bathroom, a car park or a bedroom. It needs no equipment and no explanation. Next time a craving lands, give your body a job before your mind starts negotiating.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for a body that can change how I feel in two minutes.

**Affirmations:** 43, 308, 294

## 05-03 · Sober judgement
status: approved

The most important piece of safety gear is a clear head. Helmets, ropes and life jackets all matter, and the decisions are still yours. You judge the distance, the depth, the speed of the car coming the other way. You decide whether to turn back. Alcohol and drugs blur exactly those calls, and they do it while telling you that you are fine. Sober, your judgement is your own. You notice when something feels wrong, and you can act on it. That applies on a mountain, in the surf, on the road and in a heated argument. Trust the clear voice, and listen when it says stop.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for a clear head when a decision matters.

**Affirmations:** 300, 12, 363

## 05-04 · The quiet self
status: approved

Who are you when nothing is happening? No task, no screen, no company, no drink. For a lot of people that question is uncomfortable, and drinking was partly a way of avoiding it. Sober, there are quiet stretches where you meet yourself. You find out what your mind does when it is idle. You notice what you like, what worries you, what you keep returning to. It can be awkward at first, like sitting with a stranger. Give it time. Sit on the step for ten minutes with nothing in your hands. The person you find there is someone you will be living with, and most people come to like them.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for a quiet moment with myself, and for whoever I find there.

**Affirmations:** 243, 299, 89

## 05-05 · Self-compassion
status: approved

Think of how you would treat a friend who was going through what you are going through. You would be patient. You would remind them how hard it is and how far they have come. You wouldn't call them weak for struggling. Now notice how you speak to yourself in the same situation. For many people the two voices are nothing alike. Self-compassion means closing that gap. It has nothing to do with letting yourself off. People who are kind to themselves after a setback tend to recover faster than people who attack themselves. When things are hard today, say to yourself what you would say to the friend.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can be as patient with myself as I would be with a friend.

**Affirmations:** 265, 155, 352

## 05-06 · Your worst day
status: approved

Everyone has a worst day, and for people who drank or used, it can be very bad. Something said, something done, someone hurt. That day is real and it may need answering for. It is also one day. You have lived thousands of others, and in many of them you were kind, funny, hard-working and loyal. A person is the whole record. If you judged a friend only by their lowest moment, you would know you were being unfair. Give yourself the same fairness. Take responsibility for the worst day where you can, and then let the rest of your days count as well.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I am the whole of my life and more than any one day in it.

**Affirmations:** 221, 134, 203

## 05-07 · Small wins
status: approved

Some victories are invisible to everyone else. You drove past the bottle shop without slowing. You answered a hard email. You stayed in a conversation that you wanted to leave. You went to bed when you said you would. Nobody claps for these, and that makes them easy to dismiss. Collect them. At the end of the day, name one thing you handled better than you once would have. Write it down if you like. Over time the list becomes a record of who you are turning into. On a low day, read it back. Big changes are made almost entirely of small wins.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for one small thing I got right that nobody else saw.

**Affirmations:** 268, 318, 50

## 05-08 · Regular meals
status: approved

Eat at regular times. It is plain advice, and it does a surprising amount. When meals are steady, your energy and your mood stay on a more even keel. When they are skipped, you get shaky and short-tempered, and everything feels harder than it is. Three meals at roughly the same hours each day is a good frame. They don't have to be impressive. Breakfast can be toast and fruit. The timing is doing much of the work. Set an alarm for lunch if you tend to forget. If your days have no fixed shape, meals can be the pegs that hold the rest of the routine up.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for regular meals and the steadiness they give me.

**Affirmations:** 97, 320, 230

## 05-09 · Guilt
status: approved

Guilt is uncomfortable, and it is useful. It points at a particular thing you did and says that it went against what you value. That is different from the feeling that you are rotten all through. Guilt has an address. Because it is specific, you can do something with it. Ask what it is pointing at. Then ask whether a repair is possible: an apology, a repayment, a changed habit. If it is, make a plan and carry it out when the time is right. If nothing can be done now, let the guilt shape how you act from here. Once it has delivered its message, it tends to quieten.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that my conscience works, and that it shows me where to mend things.

**Affirmations:** 127, 91, 48

## 05-10 · The first visit
status: approved

The first visit anywhere is the hardest one you will make. A support group, a gym, a class, a clinic. You don't know where to park, where to sit or what happens next. Your mind supplies a dozen reasons to turn around. Nearly everyone inside felt the same on their first day, and most of them remember it. A few things make it easier. Arrive a little early. Tell one person that you are new. Give yourself permission to just watch. Stay to the end if you can. The second visit is far easier, and by the third you will know where the cups are kept.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for every door I have walked through nervous, and for the people who made room.

**Affirmations:** 63, 110, 178

## 05-11 · Example
status: approved

How you live says more than anything you could tell people. If someone close to you is drinking too much, lectures seldom help. They have heard them. What they haven't seen, perhaps, is a person they know getting up clear, keeping their word and enjoying life without a drink. That is quietly persuasive. There is no need to mention it. Keep going where they can see you. Some will ask you about it one day, and that is the moment to talk. Until then, your ordinary sober day is doing more good than you know, for them and for anyone else who happens to be watching.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that simply living my day might help someone else.

**Affirmations:** 311, 113, 344

## 05-12 · The body's report
status: approved

Your body gives a plain report, if you ask for it. Stop for a minute and check in from head to feet. Is your jaw tight? Are your shoulders up near your ears? Is your stomach empty, or churning? Are you thirsty, tired, sore? Drinking and using taught many people to ignore these messages or drown them out. They are worth reading. A tight chest might be worry. Heavy limbs might be a need for sleep. Once you know what the report says, you can respond to the real problem. Make a habit of asking a couple of times a day. Your body has been waiting to be heard.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that my body tells me what it needs when I stop to ask.

**Affirmations:** 92, 200, 270

## 05-13 · Those who are gone
status: approved

Most people in recovery know someone who didn't make it. A friend, a parent, a person from a group who stopped turning up. Drinking and drugs take people who were loved and funny and trying hard. It is right to remember them. Say their name today. Think of something they did that made you laugh. Their death was no failure of character, and it says nothing about what will happen to you. If remembering brings grief, let it come, and share it with someone if you can. Staying sober today is one way of honouring them. So is being kind to the next person who is struggling.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the people I have lost, and for the time I had with them.

**Affirmations:** 316, 119, 174

## 05-14 · Fear of life without it
status: approved

It is common to be frightened of who you will be without it. Will you be boring? Will you cope at parties, at funerals, on bad days? Will your friends still want you around? These fears are reasonable, because you are being asked to step into a life you haven't seen yet. They ease as the evidence comes in. You get through one event, then another. You find that you can still laugh, still talk, still feel. If you are years along, you may remember this fear and smile. If you are at the start, being afraid is fine. Take the next step with the fear in your pocket.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for each thing I was scared of that turned out to be manageable.

**Affirmations:** 17, 291, 262

## 05-15 · A specific apology
status: approved

A good apology is short and exact. Say what you did, in plain words. Say that it was wrong and that you are sorry. Then stop. Leave out the reasons, the drink, the stress you were under and anything beginning with the word but. Leave out any request for forgiveness as well. The other person may need time, or may never be ready, and that is theirs to decide. A specific apology shows that you understand what happened from their side. A vague one mostly makes the speaker feel better. If there is one you owe, work out the exact sentence today. Choose your moment with care.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can name what I did and say sorry for it.

**Affirmations:** 314, 342, 37

## 05-16 · When motivation fades
status: approved

Motivation is a visitor. It turns up full of energy, stays a while, and leaves without notice. If your sobriety depends on feeling motivated, it will wobble every time the visitor goes. Habits are different. A habit runs whether or not you feel like it. You brush your teeth without a pep talk. The aim is to make the things that keep you well just as automatic: the walk, the meal, the call, the bedtime. Set them at fixed times and tie them to things you already do. On the days when you feel nothing at all, the habits will still be there to lean on.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the habits that carry me when the feeling isn't there.

**Affirmations:** 257, 204, 287

## 05-17 · Writing the reason down
status: approved

Write down why you are doing this. Do it on a day when the reason is clear to you. Be specific. Describe the morning, the conversation, the look on someone's face, the thing you never want to repeat. Add what you are hoping for. Keep the page somewhere you can reach it: in your wallet, on your phone, inside a cupboard door. Memory is unreliable. In a weak hour it will tell you that it was never that bad. The page will tell you the truth, in your own handwriting. Read it when you need to. Update it as your reasons grow.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for my reasons, and for being able to read them back.

**Affirmations:** 22, 272, 325

## 05-18 · Envy
status: approved

It can sting to watch someone enjoy a drink with no trouble at all. One glass with dinner, a beer at the game, and then they stop. You might feel envy, and a flash of unfairness. Why them and not you? That feeling is honest and it doesn't need to be hidden. It also leaves something out. You are comparing their easy evening with your whole hard history. You have no idea what they struggle with. Everyone has something they can't do safely. This happens to be yours, and living without it brings things that the easy drinker may never think to value. Let the sting pass through.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for what my own path has given me that an easier one would not have.

**Affirmations:** 133, 162, 333

## 05-19 · The wave
status: approved

An urge behaves like a wave. It starts as a swell, builds, reaches a peak and then falls away. Left alone, it does this by itself, usually within half an hour. The trouble is that at the peak it feels as if it will keep climbing forever. Every wave you have ever felt has come down. Your part is to stay where you are and let it pass under you. Breathe. Watch it build. Tell yourself that this is the top. Fighting the wave tires you out, and feeding it makes the next one bigger. Riding it leaves you where you started, still standing.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that every urge I have felt has passed.

**Affirmations:** 33, 362, 216

## 05-20 · Without a job
status: approved

Being out of work is hard on anyone. For someone staying sober, the long empty day is an extra test. Work gives a reason to get up, a place to be and people to talk to. Without it, you have to supply those yourself. Get up at a set time anyway. Shower and dress. Give the morning one task, the afternoon another. Leave the house once a day. Volunteer, study, or help someone with a job of theirs. None of this fixes the money, and the worry is real. It does keep the days from blurring, and it keeps you in shape for the work that comes next.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for a day I can give shape to, even without work.

**Affirmations:** 197, 346, 96

## 05-21 · Getting back up
status: approved

Falling is part of learning anything hard. What matters most is how soon you get back up. If you have slipped, the fastest route back is the simplest: stop, tell someone, and start again today. Waiting for the start of the month, or until you feel worthy, only makes the gap longer. You are welcome back the moment you turn around. Nobody who understands recovery will think less of you. Many of them have done the same. If you haven't fallen, the idea still applies to a missed routine or a bad week. Come back to what works. Coming back is the skill.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can always begin again, starting now.

**Affirmations:** 250, 15, 101

## 05-22 · Self-respect
status: approved

Self-respect is built, one action at a time. Nobody can hand it to you. It comes from doing what you said you would do, telling the truth when a lie would be easier, and treating people decently when nobody is checking. Each of those is a small deposit. For a while, drinking may have drained the account, because you kept doing things you couldn't stand behind. Sober, the deposits begin again. You may not notice the balance growing. Then one day you catch yourself thinking that you handled something well, and you realise you believe it. Do one thing today that you will respect tomorrow.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for one thing I did today that I can stand behind.

**Affirmations:** 233, 128, 297

## 05-23 · The surprise urge
status: approved

Some cravings make sense. You are stressed, or tired, or standing in a pub. Others turn up on a good day for no reason you can see. You are happy, things are going well, and suddenly you want a drink. It can be unsettling, as if your own mind has turned on you. Nothing of the kind has happened. The brain stored thousands of links between drinking and ordinary life, and every so often one fires at random. A surprise urge needs the same response as any other. Notice it, name it, and let it pass. If it helps, tell someone that it came out of nowhere. They will probably say theirs do too.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that an urge can surprise me and I can still stay steady.

**Affirmations:** 49, 334, 298

## 05-24 · Wild places
status: approved

Go somewhere with no signal. A beach at the end of a dirt road, a forest track, a hill outside town, a campsite by a river. Leave the phone in the bag. At first the quiet can feel like something is missing. Then your ears adjust. You hear water, birds, your own footsteps. Your thoughts slow to walking pace. Wild places ask nothing of you and judge nothing. Problems that seemed enormous in town tend to shrink out here. Go for an afternoon if that is all you have. Tell someone where you are headed, take water, and let the place do what it does.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for quiet places and the calm I find in them.

**Affirmations:** 278, 107, 258

## 05-25 · Crying
status: approved

Tears are the body letting something go. You may find that you cry more easily now, at a song, an advertisement, a kind word. You may also find that you can't cry at all for a while, and then it arrives all at once. Both are common. For years the feelings underneath were held down, and they need a way out. Crying is one of the ways. It is no sign that you are falling apart. People usually feel lighter and calmer afterwards. If tears come, let them. Find a private spot if you need one. If someone you trust is nearby, you can let them see.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can cry when I need to, and feel lighter for it.

**Affirmations:** 136, 238, 337

## 05-26 · The long out-breath
status: approved

Tension shortens your breathing and lifts it high into the chest. You can use that link in the other direction. Breathe in through your nose for a count of four. Breathe out slowly through your mouth for a count of six or eight. Do it five times. The long out-breath tells your body that the danger has passed, and your heart rate begins to settle. Nobody can see you doing it. It works in a queue, in a meeting, in the car, in bed. It leaves the problem where it was and puts you in a better state to deal with it.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I carry a way to calm myself everywhere I go.

**Affirmations:** 44, 349, 281

## 05-27 · Disappointment
status: approved

Things fall through. The job goes to someone else. The person doesn't ring. The result is worse than you hoped. Disappointment is a heavy, sinking feeling, and for a long time the reflex was to pour something over it. Sober, you have to let it land. Say plainly what you were hoping for and that it didn't happen. Tell someone. Let yourself be flat for an evening. Then ask a practical question: is there another way, or something else worth wanting? A letdown handled sober hurts, and then it ends. One that was drunk over tends to come back the next morning with company.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can be let down and get through it.

**Affirmations:** 141, 39, 189

## 05-28 · Discipline
status: approved

Discipline has a harsh sound, as if it were about punishment. Think of it as a favour to the person you will be next week. That person will wake up in the house you tidied, with the money you didn't waste and the fitness you built. They will thank you for the early night and the difficult phone call. Seen this way, discipline is a kind of care. Start small enough to succeed. One fixed bedtime. One walk. One job done before the fun. When you don't feel like it, picture next week's version of you and do it for them.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for what I did earlier that is making today easier.

**Affirmations:** 207, 88, 10

## 05-29 · Without it
status: approved

Take the drink away, and who is left? It is a fair question, and the answer takes a while to arrive. For years, drinking may have decided how you spent your evenings, who your friends were and how you saw yourself. Without it there is a gap, and a gap can be frightening. It is also room. You get to find out what you enjoy, what you believe and how you want to spend a free day. Some answers will surprise you. Try things. Keep what fits and drop what doesn't. You are meeting someone who has been there all along, waiting for some space.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the chance to find out who I am.

**Affirmations:** 214, 213, 336

## 05-30 · Rest days
status: approved

Rest is part of the work. Muscles grow on the days off. Minds sort themselves out during sleep. Recovery from anything needs pauses built in. If you are the type who throws yourself at things, you may feel guilty when you stop, as though rest were laziness. Notice that, and rest anyway. Take one day with no training, no chores list and no self-improvement. Read, nap, sit in the yard, watch something silly. Rest also keeps you from swapping drinking for overdoing everything else. You will return to your routines with more energy than if you had pushed straight through.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for permission to stop and do very little.

**Affirmations:** 103, 310, 1

## 05-31 · Empty hours
status: approved

Sobriety hands back a lot of time. The hours spent drinking, getting hold of it and recovering from it are suddenly yours. At first they can feel less like a gift and more like a hole. Evenings stretch. Free days yawn. Those hours need somewhere to go, or they tend to drift back to where they came from. Fill a few of them on purpose. A class, a project, a sport, a regular visit to someone. Leave a few empty as well, for rest. If you have been sober a long time, look at where your hours go now. They are still the most valuable thing you were given back.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the hours I have back, and for choosing how to spend them.

**Affirmations:** 153, 322, 11
