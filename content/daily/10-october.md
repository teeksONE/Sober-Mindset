# October

## 10-01 · Who you tell
status: approved

Your sobriety is your information. You decide who hears about it, when, and in how much detail. Some people tell everyone on the first day. Others tell two people and leave it there for a year. Both are fine. A useful way to sort it is by what each person needs to know. Close friends and family may need the real story. Workmates may only need to hear that you are off the drink tonight. Strangers need nothing at all. You can also tell in stages, a little more as trust builds. Nobody is owed your history. Share it where it helps you or helps them.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that my story is mine to tell in my own time.

**Affirmations:** 75, 282, 330

## 10-02 · Numbness
status: approved

Sometimes you feel nothing at all. No sadness, no joy, no interest. Things happen and they barely register. It can be more unsettling than strong emotion, because at least pain tells you that you are alive. Numbness is a phase too. It often follows a period of overload, as if a fuse has tripped to protect the wiring. It lifts in time, usually in small flickers. Meanwhile, keep doing the ordinary things: eat, move, see people, sleep. Do them without expecting to feel much. If the blankness goes on for weeks, or comes with dark thoughts, tell a doctor or counsellor. That is what they are there for.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for any flicker of feeling, and for knowing that numbness passes.

**Affirmations:** 146, 257, 71

## 10-03 · How far down
status: approved

One common story says you must lose everything before you can change. The job, the family, the house. For some people it did go that way. Nobody is required to follow that route. You can stop at any depth. If drinking is costing you sleep and self-respect and nothing else yet, that is reason enough. The earlier you turn, the less there is to rebuild. If you did lose a great deal, the same idea still applies today. Wherever you are standing is the lowest you need to go. You can put the shovel down here. The ground you are on will do for a foundation.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can stop here, with what I still have.

**Affirmations:** 8, 102, 359

## 10-04 · Fifteen minutes
status: approved

With an urge shouting at you, promise yourself only this: you will wait fifteen minutes before deciding. Set a timer if you like. In those minutes, do something that uses your hands or your legs. Wash up, walk to the corner, hang out the washing. When the timer goes, check again. Often the urge has dropped by half. If it hasn't, take another fifteen. A flat refusal gives the craving something to fight. A short delay gives it nothing, and cravings have little stamina. Most are over before the second timer ends. This is one of the simplest tools there is, and it works at any stage.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I only ever have to wait a little while.

**Affirmations:** 42, 240, 198

## 10-05 · Easily moved
status: approved

You may find yourself tearing up at an ad, a child's drawing or a stranger's kindness on the bus. A song from years ago stops you in the street. This can be embarrassing, and it can make you wonder whether you have gone soft. What has happened is that your feelings are switched back on, and they haven't settled into their adult range yet. Being easily moved is the price of feeling at all. It is a fair price. The same openness that has you blinking through a wedding speech lets you enjoy music, food and people more than you have in years. Let it happen. It evens out, and you keep the good part.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that small things can reach me.

**Affirmations:** 147, 169, 41

## 10-06 · Painful dates
status: approved

Certain dates carry old pain. The anniversary of a death. The day a relationship ended. A birthday that someone is no longer here for. The date of an accident or an arrest. You may forget it is coming until the day arrives and you find yourself low, irritable or craving without knowing why. Look ahead in the calendar and mark the ones that are yours. In the days before, tell someone that a hard one is coming. Plan something gentle for it: a visit to a meaningful place, a meal, time with a person who understands. The pain may still come. It lands more softly when you are expecting it.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the people and times those dates remind me of.

**Affirmations:** 262, 229, 98

## 10-07 · The inner critic
status: approved

Listen for the voice that comments on everything you do. It calls you an idiot. It says you always ruin things. It asks who you think you are. It speaks so often that you may have stopped noticing it as a voice and taken it for the truth. Start by catching it. When it speaks, ask two questions. Is that true? Is it useful? Usually it is an exaggeration, and it helps nothing. The critic believes it is keeping you in line, yet harshness has never made anyone steadier. There is no need to silence it. Answer it with a calmer voice, and over time that one gets louder.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for a kinder voice in my head, however quiet it still is.

**Affirmations:** 138, 25, 266

## 10-08 · Labels
status: approved

Some people find a label helpful. A word for what they are gives them clarity and a community. Other people find the same word heavy, or inaccurate, or simply not theirs. You can change your life without settling the question. Stopping drinking, seeing a doctor and joining a group are all open to you with no announcement of identity. If a word helps you, use it proudly. If it gets in the way, set it aside. You can also change your mind later. What you do each day is what changes your life. The vocabulary is optional, and it belongs to you.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can describe myself in my own words.

**Affirmations:** 28, 184, 133

## 10-09 · Friends who stay
status: approved

When you stop drinking, your friends sort themselves. Some fade when the pub is no longer on offer. Others ring and suggest breakfast. They come for a walk, a film, a kick of the footy. They ask how you are going and wait for the answer. These people are showing you who they are. It may be a different group from the one you expected. A quiet workmate can turn out to be solid, while a mate of ten years goes missing. Notice who makes the effort and make it back. Tell them you appreciate it. Friendships that survive this change tend to be the ones worth having for life.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for any friend who is glad to see me as I am.

**Affirmations:** 72, 60, 304

## 10-10 · Clearing the house
status: approved

Make your home an easy place to stay sober. Go through the cupboards, the fridge, the shed, the car and the back of the wardrobe. Pour it out or give it away. Hidden bottles count, and so does the one you were keeping for guests. It can feel dramatic, and it is simply practical. A craving that would pass in fifteen minutes becomes dangerous when the answer is four steps away. Distance buys you time, and time is what you need. If you live with people who drink, ask whether theirs can be kept out of sight. If you are years in, the same idea applies to anything else you would rather not reach for on a bad night.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for a home that helps me.

**Affirmations:** 14, 321, 92

## 10-11 · Humour
status: approved

You may worry that your sense of humour lived in the bottle, and that without a drink you will be earnest and flat. Give it a little time. The thing that made you funny was how you see the world, and that came with you. Sober, the timing gets better. You catch more, because you are listening. You remember the good lines the next day. Humour also becomes one of your best tools. Being able to laugh at a bad day, or at your own disasters, takes some of their weight away. Recovery rooms and locker rooms are full of laughter for that reason. Yours is still in there.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for my sense of humour, which was mine all along.

**Affirmations:** 226, 366, 153

## 10-12 · Until bedtime
status: approved

Shrink a hard day to the hours you have left. Forget next month. Forget tomorrow. How long until you can reasonably go to bed? Four hours, perhaps. That is all you are doing. Break it down further if you need to: dinner, a shower, something to watch, then lights out. Anyone can do four hours. When your head hits the pillow, the day is banked and nothing can take it back. People sober for decades still use this on the bad ones. Tomorrow you can think about tomorrow. Tonight has one finish line, and it is close.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that the finish line for today is only a few hours away.

**Affirmations:** 11, 118, 209

## 10-13 · Nights in
status: approved

A quiet night at home can feel like a punishment at first. Everyone else is out, and you are on the couch. Give it a few goes. Make the place comfortable. Cook something good, or order the meal you love. Line up a film, a series, a book, a game. Have a bath. Ring someone for a long talk. Go to bed when you are tired, with clean teeth and a clear head. In the morning you will have slept properly and spent very little. Many people come to guard these evenings. A night in is a way of looking after tomorrow, and it has its own pleasures.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for a quiet night and a comfortable place to spend it.

**Affirmations:** 178, 220, 364

## 10-14 · Courage
status: approved

Courage is doing the thing while you are still afraid. If you waited for the fear to go first, you would wait forever. Look at what you have already done. You admitted something you had hidden. You walked into a room of strangers. You said no at a table where everyone else said yes. You were scared each time, and you did it. That is the real article. Brave people feel fear as much as anyone. They have just practised acting alongside it. Pick one thing you have been avoiding, and take the first small step today, shaking if necessary.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for every time I have acted while afraid.

**Affirmations:** 150, 164, 349

## 10-15 · Looking ahead
status: approved

Once in a while, look over the next couple of weeks. Where are the risky spots? A wedding, a work function, a visit from an old drinking mate, a trip away, a date that always gets to you. Most trouble can be seen coming if you look. For each one, decide a few things in advance. Will you go? How long will you stay? What will you drink? Who knows you might need a call? Put the plans in your phone. Then stop thinking about it. Having scanned ahead, you can relax into the ordinary days, knowing nothing is going to ambush you.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for being able to see what is coming and prepare for it.

**Affirmations:** 174, 39, 335

## 10-16 · One honest conversation
status: approved

Sometimes one conversation changes everything. You have been carrying something for weeks: a worry, a resentment, a secret, a thing you need to ask for. It has grown in the silence and started to colour your days. Then you sit down with the person and say it. Ten minutes later the weight has gone, or at least it has become something two people are holding. Think about what you are avoiding saying, and to whom. Pick a calm time. Start with the simplest true sentence you can find. It may go better than you fear. Even if it is hard, you will no longer be carrying it alone.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the relief of saying what needed to be said.

**Affirmations:** 85, 149, 236

## 10-17 · Company
status: approved

Some company makes drinking feel like the entry fee. Around certain people, a glass in your hand is how you show that you belong. Nobody says it. You just feel it. These may be people you love. Notice which groups have this effect on you, and how strong it is. For a while you might see them in different settings: breakfast in place of the pub, one on one in place of the whole crew. Some will adjust happily. With others, the friendship may turn out to have been mostly the drinking. Belonging that costs you your sobriety is too expensive. Look for company where turning up is enough.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for people I can be around without having to pay for it.

**Affirmations:** 37, 329, 173

## 10-18 · Sleep takes time
status: approved

Sleep often gets worse before it gets better. Alcohol knocks you out, and without it you may lie awake, wake at odd hours or dream vividly. It is discouraging, because you stopped in order to feel better. Your body is relearning how to fall asleep under its own power, and that takes weeks for most people, sometimes longer. Help it with regular hours, some exercise and a dark, quiet room. Be patient on the bad nights. Natural sleep, when it comes, does you more good than the old kind did. If sleeplessness drags on, talk to a doctor. Meanwhile, each night is practice.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for whatever sleep I got, and for my body learning to rest.

**Affirmations:** 93, 23, 52

## 10-19 · The driver
status: approved

Somebody has to get everyone home, and tonight it can be you. Being the driver gives you a role at any event. You have a reason to be on soft drinks that nobody questions. You get people to their doors in one piece. Parents sleep because you are behind the wheel. It is a real service, and people are grateful. Set your own terms so it stays a pleasure: say when you are leaving, and how many you can take. You are a friend doing a favour, and nobody's taxi. Notice how good it feels to pull into your own driveway clear-headed, with everyone delivered safely.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can get the people I care about home safely.

**Affirmations:** 347, 206, 100

## 10-20 · Stretching
status: approved

Get down on the floor for ten minutes. Stretch your hamstrings, your hips, your back, your neck and shoulders. Go slowly and breathe into the tight spots. You will probably find tension you didn't know was there. Bodies store stress in the muscles, and years of hard living leave a lot of it behind. As you stretch, some of that lets go, and your mind tends to follow. It is a good thing to do first thing in the morning, or before bed, or in the middle of a craving. No equipment, no skill and no fitness are needed. Your body will tell you where to spend the time.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for ten minutes on the floor and a body that loosens.

**Affirmations:** 110, 256, 343

## 10-21 · Music
status: approved

Music is tied to memory, and some of your songs are tied to drinking. A certain album brings back a certain bar. That can make listening risky for a while. You can keep the songs. Hear them in new settings, on a run or while cooking, until they collect new associations. Meanwhile, build a soundtrack for the life you have now. Find new artists. Go back to what you loved at fifteen. Make a playlist for mornings and one for hard evenings. Go to a gig sober and hear every note. Music can lift a mood faster than almost anything. Use it on purpose.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for music, old and new.

**Affirmations:** 163, 103, 201

## 10-22 · Hard feedback
status: approved

Someone tells you something you don't want to hear. Your work wasn't good enough. You hurt them. You have been difficult lately. The first reaction is a hot flush and a wish to defend yourself or collapse. Try to do neither for a moment. Breathe. Say that you have heard it and need time to think. Later, when you are calm, look for the part that is true. There is usually some. Take that part and leave the rest. Being able to hear criticism without falling apart or fighting back is a sign of real steadiness. One person's comment on one thing you did is a long way from a verdict on you.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can hear something hard and stay on my feet.

**Affirmations:** 271, 309, 119

## 10-23 · The toast
status: approved

At weddings, birthdays and farewells, the moment comes when everyone lifts a glass. If yours has sparkling water in it, the toast works exactly the same. What you are raising is your goodwill. Nobody inspects the contents. Get your drink sorted before the speeches so you aren't caught empty-handed while trays of champagne go past. Something with bubbles in a nice glass feels right in the hand. Look at the person being honoured and mean what you say. You are fully part of the moment. If anything, you will remember the speech, the faces and the feeling better than most people in the room.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can celebrate other people wholeheartedly.

**Affirmations:** 337, 185, 307

## 10-24 · A late start
status: approved

You may be starting something later than you meant to. A degree at thirty. An apprenticeship at forty. A first real job, a change of career, a course you dropped out of years ago. It is easy to feel like the old one in the room. Look at what you bring. You know what you want now. You have survived things your classmates haven't faced. You don't waste time. Late starters are often the most determined people in any group, because they chose it with their eyes open. The years will pass either way. In five of them you can be qualified or you can still be wishing.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that it is still possible to begin.

**Affirmations:** 196, 51, 114

## 10-25 · Doing less
status: approved

Skipping things is allowed. The extra shift, the third social event in a row, the favour you don't have time for, the party you are dreading. Sobriety takes energy, especially early, and that energy has to come from somewhere. Saying no to some things is how you say yes to staying well. Look at the days ahead and find one thing that can go. Cancel it kindly and keep the explanation short. Use the time to rest or to do nothing in particular. The world carries on. People who do a bit less, and do it well, usually last longer than people who try to do it all.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for permission to do less.

**Affirmations:** 354, 319, 156

## 10-26 · What it is for
status: approved

Time and money both come back when you stop. The question is what they are for. Without an answer, they leak away into small things and you wonder where they went. Sit down and decide. What do you want your spare hours to build? What do you want your spare dollars to do? It might be a trip, a qualification, a safer home, time with your kids, getting out of debt, helping someone. There is no correct answer, only yours. Write down two or three things. Then, when a free evening or a little extra cash turns up, you already know where it belongs.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for having time and money to direct, in whatever amount.

**Affirmations:** 213, 341, 76

## 10-27 · Your own tastes
status: approved

What do you like? It sounds simple, and you may not know. For years, drinking decided the venue, the company and the hour, and your own preferences never got a look in. Now you get to find out. Are you a morning person or a night one? Do you like crowds or small groups? Spicy food or plain? Loud music or silence? The sea or the hills? Try both sides and pay attention to how you feel afterwards. Some answers will surprise you. Build your days around what you learn. A life arranged to suit the person living it is much easier to stay sober in.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for finding out what I enjoy.

**Affirmations:** 242, 30, 283

## 10-28 · Promises to yourself
status: approved

Make yourself a small promise and keep it. Nobody else needs to know. You will drink a glass of water before coffee. You will walk at lunch. You will be in bed by eleven. Then do it, today, exactly as stated. A promise kept in private does something that public praise can't. It teaches the oldest part of you that your word is good. For a long time you may have broken promises to yourself daily, and stopped believing them. Belief comes back through small ones, kept. Keep tomorrow's as well. After a few weeks you will notice that when you tell yourself something, you listen.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for one promise I made to myself and kept.

**Affirmations:** 203, 101, 279

## 10-29 · Feeling cured
status: approved

A dangerous moment can come dressed as a good one. You feel great. The cravings have gone. Life is working. A thought arrives that the problem is behind you and the precautions can be dropped. You stop doing the things that got you here, one by one. Nothing happens at first, which seems to prove the point. Feeling well is the result of what you have been doing, and it depends on your carrying on. Enjoy the good stretch fully. Keep the basics in place while you do. Many people find that the times they felt most certain were the times they needed to pay the most attention.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for feeling well, and for the habits that keep me that way.

**Affirmations:** 252, 276, 16

## 10-30 · Teaching a beginner
status: approved

Show someone how to do a thing you have learned. Hold the rope for a first-time climber. Teach a friend to catch a wave, change a tyre, bake bread, do a push-up properly. As you explain the basics, you see how much you now know without thinking. Steps that once baffled you have become automatic. Teaching also sharpens your own skill, because you have to work out why it works. It is a generous way to spend an afternoon, and the other person will remember who got them started. Being an expert is unnecessary. One step ahead, and patient, is plenty.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for having something worth teaching.

**Affirmations:** 325, 96, 344

## 10-31 · Room for more
status: approved

When drinking goes, it leaves a space. At first the space can look like loss: empty evenings, a missing ritual, a gap in your social life. It is also room. Something good can go there, and you get to choose what. A sport, a relationship, a course, a creative project, a cause, a quieter mind. If you fill it with nothing, old habits tend to drift back in. So be deliberate. Ask what you have always wanted more of, and start making a place for it. On a night when many people are out drinking, you could be planning what goes in the room.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the room in my life, and for choosing what fills it.

**Affirmations:** 275, 88, 217
