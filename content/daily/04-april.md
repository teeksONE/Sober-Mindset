# April

## 04-01 · Feelings return
status: approved

When nothing is turning the volume down, feelings come back loud. Irritation, sadness, tenderness, embarrassment, delight. Some arrive that you haven't met in years. It can be alarming, as if something has gone wrong with you. What has happened is that the damping has been taken off. You are feeling at full strength, perhaps for the first time as an adult. It takes practice to carry that. Go easy. You don't have to act on a feeling or understand it straight away. Let it rise, notice where it sits in your body, and let it fall. Over time the loudness evens out and the range becomes something you value.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can feel things fully, even when it is a lot.

**Affirmations:** 122, 161, 30

## 04-02 · The short no
status: approved

Turning down a drink takes only a few words. A plain no thanks does the job. You may have a long explanation ready: the reasons, the history, the apology. Hardly anyone needs it. Most offers are simple politeness, and a simple answer closes them. A short no also sounds sure of itself, which makes a second offer less likely. Long explanations invite debate. Practise your line until it comes out without effort. Say it in the mirror if that helps. Have a follow-up ready that changes the subject, such as asking the other person a question. The less you make of it, the less everyone else will.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for how simple a no can be.

**Affirmations:** 76, 10, 233

## 04-03 · Overwhelm
status: approved

Some days the list is too long to look at. Work, bills, messages, family, your own head. Everything seems to need doing at once, and the weight of it makes it hard to start anything. When that happens, stop looking at the whole list. Pick the next single task, and make it small. Wash one cup. Answer one email. Put your shoes on. Do that, then choose the next. Overwhelm is a feeling about the total. It fades when your attention is on one thing your hands can do. You may get through less than you hoped. You will get through more than if you had stayed frozen.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the next small thing, and that it is all I have to do right now.

**Affirmations:** 135, 164, 115

## 04-04 · Naming it
status: approved

When an urge arrives, give it its name. Say to yourself, plainly, that this is a craving. It seems too simple to matter. It matters because, unnamed, an urge feels like a fact about what you need. Named, it becomes an event that is happening to you, like a headache or a loud noise next door. You can watch an event. You can wait for it to finish. Naming gives you a small step back, and that step is often enough room to choose. You can add detail if it helps: how strong it is, where you feel it, what set it off. Then carry on with what you were doing.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the small distance that comes from calling a craving what it is.

**Affirmations:** 34, 85, 157

## 04-05 · Play
status: approved

Do something today for no reason at all. Kick a ball against a wall. Draw badly. Build something pointless. Throw a frisbee, skim stones, play a video game with a mate, dance in the kitchen. Adults forget how to play, and people in recovery can be especially serious, because so much has been at stake. Play has no goal and no score that matters. It lets your mind rest in a way that effort never does. If it feels silly, you are probably doing it right. Half an hour of it can change the colour of a whole day. You have permission to enjoy yourself.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for anything I did just because I felt like it.

**Affirmations:** 176, 250, 136

## 04-06 · Luck and effort
status: approved

Two things have brought you this far: luck and effort. Luck is the help that turned up, the person who said the right thing, the near miss that could have gone the other way. Effort is everything you did with it. Both deserve credit. If you only count luck, you sell yourself short and start to feel that it could all vanish. If you only count effort, you can become hard on people who haven't had your breaks. Hold the two together. Be thankful for what you were handed, and proud of what you did. Then spend a little of your good fortune on someone else.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the luck I have had, and for what I did with it.

**Affirmations:** 208, 158, 59

## 04-07 · Lowering the bar
status: approved

In a hard stretch, lower the bar until you can step over it. Forget the exercise plan, the tidy house, the perfect diet and the long list. On the worst days there is one job: get to bed sober. Everything else is extra. This is a sensible use of limited strength, and nothing to feel guilty about. People who have stayed sober for decades have had days exactly like this. They ate what was in the cupboard, cancelled what they could, and went to bed early. Tomorrow you can raise the bar a notch if you feel up to it. Tonight, sober is enough.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that on a hard day, one thing is enough.

**Affirmations:** 20, 81, 251

## 04-08 · Repetition
status: approved

You become what you do again and again. One run doesn't make a runner, and one sober day can feel like a small thing. Keep doing it, and at some point the description fits. Nobody announces the change. You just notice that reaching for a glass of water has become ordinary, that mornings start a certain way, that people have begun to describe you differently. Identity follows action, a little behind. There is no need to feel like a different person today. Repeating the actions of the person you are becoming is enough. The feeling arrives later.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for each ordinary action that is slowly shaping who I am.

**Affirmations:** 237, 150, 72

## 04-09 · Nowhere to go
status: approved

There are days when everyone else seems to have somewhere to be. A public holiday, a big game, a night when the streets are full. If you have no invitation, the quiet can feel like a verdict on your life. It is a circumstance, and it passes when the day ends. Plan it as you would plan any risky day. Decide in advance what you will eat, what you will watch or read, and who you could ring. Get outside for part of it. Look for a community meal, an online group or an event that is open to anyone. Plenty of other people are alone today as well. One of them might be glad to hear from you.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for my own company, and for the other people who are on their own today too.

**Affirmations:** 351, 74, 224

## 04-10 · Muted pleasure
status: approved

For a while, the things that should feel good may feel like very little. Food is fine. Music is fine. A day out is fine. Nothing is wrong, and nothing sparkles. This flatness is common after stopping, and it can come back in patches. The part of the brain that handles reward has been pushed hard, and it takes time to find its natural level again. Knowing that helps, because the flat feeling can whisper that sober life will always be grey. That is the flatness talking. Pleasure tends to return gradually, in small moments first: a meal that tastes great, a song that lands, a laugh that surprises you. Keep doing ordinary good things while you wait.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for any small moment that felt good, however faint.

**Affirmations:** 105, 23, 260

## 04-11 · Being wrong
status: approved

When you get something wrong, say so quickly. A sharp word, a forgotten promise, a mistake at work. The longer it sits, the larger it grows, and the more energy goes into defending it. A prompt admission is short. Name what you did, say sorry if that is called for, and offer to put it right. Then stop talking. You don't need to punish yourself in front of anyone. Owning a mistake promptly is a skill that drinking tends to wear away, because there was always so much to cover. Sober, your errors are smaller and easier to face. Clear one today, if there is one waiting.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can say I was wrong and still think well of myself.

**Affirmations:** 151, 68, 95

## 04-12 · Back to basics
status: approved

When things start to wobble, go back to basics. You will know the wobble. Sleep slips, meals get skipped, you stop returning calls, and small problems start to look large. Before you search for a complicated explanation, check three things. Have you been sleeping? Have you been eating? Have you spoken properly to another person lately? Fix whichever is missing, starting tonight. It can seem too simple for how bad you feel. These three sit underneath everything else, and when one goes, the rest shakes. People many years sober return to them again and again. There is no stage at which you outgrow them.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the simple things that steady me whenever I return to them.

**Affirmations:** 263, 296, 70

## 04-13 · The pause
status: approved

Between wanting something and reaching for it, there is a gap. At first it is so short you may not see it at all. The urge comes, and your hand is already moving. With practice the gap grows. You notice the wanting half a second sooner, then a few seconds, then long enough to take a breath. In that space you have a choice that wasn't visible before. The pause can't be forced into being. You find it by paying attention, over and over, to the moment just before you act. Try it with small things today: the phone, the biscuit tin, a sharp reply. The skill carries across.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for every moment I noticed before I acted.

**Affirmations:** 58, 187, 124

## 04-14 · Wondering is allowed
status: approved

You are free to wonder about your drinking or using without deciding anything. Questions are safe. Is this costing more than it gives? Would life be easier without it? What would a month off be like? Asking doesn't commit you to an answer. Many people circle these questions for a long time, and the circling is part of how change begins. If you stopped long ago, wondering still has a place. You can ask whether your routines suit you now, or what you want from the next few years. Give yourself room to be curious. Honest questions, asked without pressure, tend to find their own answers.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can ask honest questions about my own life.

**Affirmations:** 4, 244, 106

## 04-15 · Stress
status: approved

Stress used to have one exit. A hard day ended with a drink, and the body learned to expect it. The stress still comes, and now it needs somewhere else to go. Physical outlets work fastest, because stress is a physical state. Walk quickly. Lift something. Swim. Clean the house with some force. After that, talking helps, and so does writing the problem down and choosing one thing to do about it. Sitting still and stewing tends to make it worse. Build yourself a short list of exits and keep it where you can find it. When the pressure rises, pick one and go.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the ways I have to let pressure out.

**Affirmations:** 139, 8, 302

## 04-16 · Travel
status: approved

Travel is better when you are there for all of it. You see the town in the morning, when the bakeries open and the streets are being washed. You remember the name of the person who gave you directions. You notice the food, the buildings, the sound of another language. You come home with the trip intact, where once there might have been a blur of bars that looked like the bars at home. Travelling sober takes a little planning. Know where you will be in the evenings. Pack what steadies you. Find out where support is, in case you want it. Then go somewhere you have always wanted to see.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for every place I have seen clearly, near or far.

**Affirmations:** 286, 239, 5

## 04-17 · Hosting
status: approved

Having people over puts you in charge. You choose the time, the food, the music and what is in the fridge. That makes hosting one of the easier ways to be social while sober. A breakfast, a barbecue lunch, a games night, a pot of something on the stove. Put out drinks that are interesting and happen to have no alcohol in them. Decide beforehand whether guests can bring their own, and tell them plainly. Set an end time if you want one. People mostly remember whether they felt welcome and well fed. You can give them that with a clear head, and enjoy your own party.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for a place where I can welcome people on my own terms.

**Affirmations:** 338, 154, 312

## 04-18 · The exception
status: approved

You may often be the only one in the room doing things differently. The one with the soft drink. The one who leaves early, or gets up early, or says no to the second venue. At first this can feel exposed, as though everyone is looking. Mostly they are thinking about themselves. Over time, being the exception gets comfortable, and then it becomes something like freedom. You are no longer steering by what the group does. Some people will quietly admire it. A few may ask how you manage. There is nothing to explain or defend. Carry on being the one who does it your way.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can do things my own way and be at ease with it.

**Affirmations:** 225, 305, 142

## 04-19 · Ten quiet minutes
status: approved

At any gathering, you can step outside for ten minutes. Nobody will mind and few will notice. Stand in the yard, sit in the car, walk to the corner and back. Breathe slowly. Check how you are: hungry, tired, tense, fine? Send a message to someone if you want to. Ten minutes away from the noise lets your shoulders drop and your thinking clear. Then decide whether to go back in or call it a night. Either is a good answer. Use the break before you need it badly. A short pause in the middle of an event can make the difference between enduring it and enjoying it.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for ten quiet minutes whenever I need them.

**Affirmations:** 350, 317, 180

## 04-20 · Small things
status: approved

Look at what is working. Hot water comes out of the tap. The car starts. There is food in the cupboard and a bed to sleep in. Your legs carry you up the stairs. The kettle boils. None of this is dramatic, and all of it can be lost, which some people know from experience. Noticing small things is a skill, and it grows with use. It works on bad days too, because a bad day still contains a hot shower. Pick three small things that are going right and name them, out loud or on paper. It takes a minute and it shifts the weight of the day.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for hot water, a bed, and anything else that quietly worked.

**Affirmations:** 319, 7, 228

## 04-21 · Debt
status: approved

Money owed is heavy, and the usual response is to stop opening the mail. If that is where you are, start by finding out the full number. Write down every debt and what it costs each month. Seeing the total is frightening for a moment and then, oddly, a relief, because a known number can be worked on. Pay the smallest or the most urgent first. Ring the people you owe and tell them the truth. Many will set up a payment plan. Free financial counselling services exist for exactly this. Each bill dealt with is a weight off, and you will feel each one go.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can face what I owe and take one step toward clearing it.

**Affirmations:** 185, 182, 290

## 04-22 · Being known
status: approved

Let one person know how you really are. Most people answer the question with fine, and move on. Somewhere in your life it helps to have a person who gets the true answer, including on the bad days. That means saying when you are struggling, flat, tempted or scared, before it becomes an emergency. It feels risky. What usually follows is relief, and a closer friendship. Being known is one of the strongest protections there is, because trouble finds it hard to grow in the open. Choose someone who has earned it. Next time they ask how you are, give them the honest version.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for anyone I can give an honest answer to.

**Affirmations:** 83, 276, 269

## 04-23 · One thing at a time
status: approved

Give one task your whole attention. Close the other tabs. Put the phone in another room. Do the washing-up and only the washing-up. A scattered mind is tiring, and it leaves you with the sense of having been busy all day while finishing nothing. Doing one thing at a time is slower in the moment and faster overall. It is also calmer. Your thoughts have a single place to be. Drinking often went with a restless, divided attention that never settled. Sober, you can train it to stay put. Choose a task that takes twenty minutes, and stay with it until it is done.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the calm of doing one thing properly.

**Affirmations:** 211, 62, 275

## 04-24 · Watching others drink
status: approved

At some stage you will watch a room of people drink while you stay sober. It is an education. Early in the night, little is different. Then voices rise, stories repeat, and someone gets teary or sharp. You see what you could never see from inside it. Some people have two and go home. Others can't stop, and you may recognise them with a jolt. Try to watch without feeling superior and without envy. They are doing what you once did. What you learn is for you: how the night really goes, and how little you are missing after ten o'clock. Then go home and sleep well.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for seeing things clearly, and for the good sleep that follows.

**Affirmations:** 348, 163, 255

## 04-25 · Any age
status: approved

There is no wrong age to change. At nineteen it can seem too early, as if you haven't earned a problem yet. At sixty it can seem too late, as if the pattern has set. Neither is true. A young person who stops has decades of clear years ahead. An older person who stops can gain health, repaired relationships and peace, starting now. The best age to do this is the age you are, because it is the only one available. People have turned their lives around in their teens and in their eighties. Whatever today's date says about you, the door is open.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that it is never too early or too late for me.

**Affirmations:** 223, 323, 186

## 04-26 · Cheap thrills
status: approved

The best days often cost almost nothing. A swim at a free beach or river. A hike to a lookout. A bike ride to the next suburb. A picnic made from what is in the fridge. Climbing a hill to watch the sun come up. Drinking was expensive, and it taught you that fun has to be bought. That lesson was wrong. Fun mostly needs a bit of effort, a bit of planning and sometimes a friend. Keep a list of free things within reach of where you live. When boredom or restlessness arrives, choose one. You will come home tired, pleased and no poorer.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for good things that cost nothing.

**Affirmations:** 288, 42, 69

## 04-27 · Each one teaches
status: approved

Every urge you ride out teaches your brain something. It predicted a drink, and the drink didn't come. One lesson doesn't change much. Many lessons do. The predictions get weaker and less frequent, and the gaps between them grow. That is why each craving you get through matters, even if it felt awful and nobody saw. You were doing the central work of recovery in those minutes. If you have slipped along the way, the lessons from the urges you did ride out are still learned. Next time one arrives, remind yourself what is happening. You are teaching your brain a new habit, one wave at a time.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for each craving I have outlasted, and what it taught me.

**Affirmations:** 55, 131, 227

## 04-28 · Old consequences
status: approved

The past can send bills long after you have changed. A fine, a court date, a damaged friendship, a mark on your record, a health problem. It can feel unfair to be paying for things done by a version of you that you have left behind. Dealing with them is part of leaving that version behind. Take them one at a time. Find out exactly what is required. Ask for help with forms and deadlines. Turn up to appointments. Each consequence you face is one fewer waiting for you. Handling them sober, step by step, is solid proof to yourself of who you are now.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I can deal with my past one piece at a time.

**Affirmations:** 202, 266, 285

## 04-29 · Gear
status: approved

You need far less gear than you think. Every activity has a shop full of equipment and a voice saying you can't begin without it. You can. Running needs shoes you already own. Swimming needs togs. Climbing gyms hire everything. Most clubs lend to beginners. Buying a pile of equipment can feel like starting, while the real start is turning up. Borrow, hire, or use what is in the shed. After a month or two you will know what you really need, and you will buy better because of it. Begin with what you have, today.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful that I already have enough to begin.

**Affirmations:** 295, 274, 26

## 04-30 · Drinking friends
status: approved

Some friendships were held together by the drink. You shared a bar, a routine, a thousand laughs, and when you stop, there is suddenly little to say. That hurts, and it deserves to be taken seriously. Those people mattered to you. Some of them will adapt, and you will find new things to do together. Others will drift, and a few may take your sobriety as a comment on their own drinking. You don't have to cut anyone off in anger. You can step back kindly and leave the door open. Let yourself be sad about it. Losing a friend is a loss, whatever held you together.

**Gratitude:** Today I'm grateful for the good times I had with old friends, and for the room I now have for new ones.

**Affirmations:** 71, 358, 130
